First Chair: Is It Worth Beating the Lift Waits?

Getting to the lift before it opens costs you sleep, a cold twenty minutes and sometimes a parking fee. It buys you the emptiest, best groomed hour of the day. Here is when that trade is worth it at the big Colorado resorts, how early to show up, and what parking really costs.

What the first hour actually buys you

Here is the honest version. In the first hour the groomers are still corduroy, the chairs load without anyone shoving, and you can ski a pitch without a snowboarder sitting in the middle of it. By the time the people who ate a real breakfast arrive, the base lifts start to feel like a grocery store on a Sunday.

The first chair is not really about the first run. It is about riding the busiest lifts while they are empty, so you have your best laps in before the wait builds. Then you move. Once the main base lifts fill up, head for the outer chairs or the far side of the mountain. That trick works at every big resort, and it is the whole reason an early start pays off.

Early season makes first chair matter more

Most of Colorado is in the thin early-season stretch right now, and that changes the math. Vail opened Nov. 13 with two lifts running, Gondola One and Mountaintop Express, from 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., with intermediate skiing on Ramshorn. Copper opened Nov. 8, two days late, with American Eagle turning at 9 a.m. When a whole resort funnels through one or two lifts, a wait forms fast and it stays. See the Vail Resorts release and Summit Daily for the details.

Here is where Colorado stands in the PeakWait app today. Arapahoe Basin, Copper, Loveland and Winter Park are confirmed running. Beaver Creek opens Nov. 25, and Aspen Mountain and Snowmass open Nov. 26, all announced by the resorts. Steamboat is only our estimate for around Nov. 22, so wait for the resort to say it. Thanksgiving is Nov. 26, so expect the first real wave of crowds soon.

One flag. Summit Daily reported that Breckenridge and Copper both pushed back their Nov. 6 openings because of warm overnight temperatures. The app shows Breckenridge open for the season, but check the resort's site for which lifts are spinning before you set an alarm.

How early to arrive at the big resorts

Every resort posts different hours, and early season can trim them. Check the resort's site the night before. Here is what the resorts themselves say.

  • Breckenridge: The resort's guest page lists lifts opening at 8:30 a.m. Its parking page says to arrive before 8 a.m. and notes overflow lots with a free shuttle on busy weekends and holidays. That is half an hour of cushion. Double it on a holiday weekend.
  • Copper: The first lift on opening day turned at 9 a.m. The free Alpine and Far East lots are open from 5 a.m., so you can be early. Being there at 5 does not make it a good idea.
  • Winter Park: The resort says parking fills early on weekends, especially after fresh snow. The Mary Jane lots fill first, so read the electronic sign as you come down Berthoud Pass. G-Lot fills later, so you are more likely to find a space there.
  • Vail: Gondola One opened at 9 a.m. on opening day. The free outlying lots with bus service list hours of 7 a.m. to 11 p.m., so a free spot before 7 is not on the menu.

My rule of thumb. On a weekday, be parked about 30 minutes before the lifts. On a weekend or a powder day, give yourself an hour or more. On a holiday, stop negotiating with yourself and set the alarm.

The parking math at Vail

Vail is the cleanest example because the rules are posted. The Town of Vail's winter paid parking started today, Nov. 20. According to Discover Vail, the first hour in the Vail Village and Lionshead structures is free. After that you pay by the hour until you hit a daily cap, and the cap is higher on peak days. Cars that enter after 3 p.m. and leave before 4 a.m. park free. Check Discover Vail for the current rates before you drive up.

  • Non-peak day: Park at 8:30 and ski until 12:30. That is four hours, one free and three paid, and by then you have reached the daily cap. Ski until the lifts close and you pay the same. The cap makes your afternoon free.
  • Peak day: The cap is higher, and you do not reach it until about the sixth hour. Every extra hour until then adds to the bill, so ski your best four hours and go home.
  • Free lots: The outlying lots cost nothing and run on the town bus. You pay in time, not money.

The takeaway is that arriving early does not cost you more. The meter starts when you park, but the cap means a longer day is cheaper per hour. Bring your patience instead.

Copper is a time problem, not a money problem. The Alpine and Far East lots are free daily, and the Ten Mile, Chapel, Beeler and Union Creek lots are free after 2 p.m. Winter Park has free lots and its parking page says the shuttles are free. Breckenridge has a free satellite lot off Airport Road with a shuttle to the base areas.

When sleeping in wins

First chair is not always worth it. On a quiet weekday with nothing in the forecast, arriving at 9:30 costs you almost nothing. And if the lots fill early, afternoons are a real plan. Copper's village lots are free after 2 p.m. A local lodging company says Winter Park spaces start to open up after 2 p.m. when the early birds head home. Vail's garages are free for cars entering after 3 p.m., although the lifts were closing at 3:30 on opening day, so that is a very free and very short lap.

Go early when one or two lifts are running, when it is a weekend or holiday, or when snow fell overnight. Sleep in when none of those are true.

What to actually do

Keep it simple. Here is the plan I would use.

  • Night before: Check the resort's site for opening time and which lifts are running. Pick your first two chairs, and choose a lot that matches the day. Weekend means early, weekday means relaxed.
  • At the base: Open PeakWait and see which lifts are open and how fresh the latest wait reports are. Ride the busiest lift first while it is empty, then let the app point you to the best next chair.
  • Around 10:30: When the base lifts fill, look for a lift reporting Moving or Short, and report the wait yourself. It is free. If you are choosing between resorts for the day, PeakWait Plus ranks nearby resorts by snow and waits.

FAQ

Is getting first chair worth it?

On weekends, holidays, powder days and early in the season when only one or two lifts run, yes. Those are the days a wait builds fast. On a quiet weekday you can arrive at the opening bell and lose very little.

How early should I get to a ski resort?

Breckenridge's own parking page says to arrive before 8 a.m. for lifts that open at 8:30. I would plan about 30 minutes before the lifts on weekdays and an hour or more on weekends and powder days. Check the resort's site for current hours.

Does arriving early cost more for parking?

Not at Vail. The first hour is free, then an hourly rate applies until a daily cap, and the cap is higher on peak days. Because of the cap, a longer day costs less per hour. Copper's Alpine and Far East lots are free.

What should I do when the base lifts get busy?

Move away from the base. Head to outer chairs or the far side of the mountain, and check which lifts are reporting Moving or Short in PeakWait. You can report waits there for free too.

Can I trust the opening time I read online?

Treat it as a plan. Breckenridge and Copper both delayed Nov. 6 openings this year because of warm temperatures. Check the resort's site on the morning you go.
