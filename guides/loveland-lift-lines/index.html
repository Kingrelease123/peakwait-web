Loveland Lift Lines: How Waits Work at Denver's Closest Big Mountain

By Herb Sendit · 5 min read · November 6, 2026

Loveland sits 53 miles from Denver at exit 216, just before the Eisenhower Tunnel, and that location is most of its charm. You get 1,800 acres, free parking and a reputation for waits that stay short. Here is how the lifts split up, what Chair 1 is now, when to send beginners to the Valley, and when the calm gets tested.

The first big mountain off I-70

Loveland is 53 miles west of Denver at exit 216, and the resort points out that it comes before the Eisenhower Tunnel. That means you skip the tunnel and the traffic on the far side. If you have ever sat in a Saturday crawl toward Summit County, you know why that matters before you even touch a lift.

The mountain is two areas. Loveland Basin has the big terrain, and Loveland Valley is the gentle beginner zone. The resort lists 1,800 acres in total, and it says the whole place is built around skiing at a fair price rather than a village and a gondola. PeakWait tracks 11 lifts here, including Chet's Dream, Lift 2, Lift 3, Lift 4, Lift 6, Lift 7, Lift 8, Lift 9 and the Ptarmigan Lift.

Is Loveland open yet?

Not as far as we can tell. PeakWait shows Loveland as expected to open around Nov 1 with lifts not running yet. That is our estimate only, not a date from the resort, so do not drive up on it.

On Nov 5, Unofficial Networks reported that Loveland still had no confirmed opening day. The resort's own site said it hoped to open between late October and early November, weather and snowmaking permitting. Until skiloveland.com says lifts are turning, treat it as closed.

For a sense of the pattern, last season Loveland opened Nov 8 with Chet's Dream loading at 8:30 a.m. The Valley followed on Nov 21. If you want something that is running now, Arapahoe Basin shows as open for the season with lifts confirmed running. One more flag: the app shows Breckenridge as open, but Unofficial Networks reported on Nov 5 that Breckenridge postponed its Nov 6 opening. Check the resort's site before you go.

Chair 1 is Chet's Dream now

People still say Chair 1 out of habit. The old Lift 1 was a fixed-grip triple from 1981, and in 2018 it was replaced by a high-speed quad named Chet's Dream. Chet Upham was a Loveland partner who pushed for the area's first chairlift back in the 1950s.

Chet's Dream is the only high-speed chair at the Basin. Powder7 timed the ride at around 210 seconds for nearly 1,000 vertical feet. That is why a full chair can disappear in a hurry, and why it is the hot-lap lift. It is also the lift that carries opening-week crowds, since last season's opening terrain was top to bottom on Catwalk, Mambo and Home Run.

The rest of the Basin runs on slower chairs, and that shapes where you go.

  • Lift 2: a fixed-grip triple from 1985 and the low, slow ride from the base up to just below mid-mountain. The general manager has said its top is one of the most congested spots on the mountain. This is its last season, because it is being replaced in summer 2027 by a second high-speed chair called Forest Meadow.
  • Ptarmigan Lift: the way to South Chutes, a blue zone known for good snow.
  • Lifts 4 and 8: the advanced and expert zones, which are the best places to ski away from the Chet's Dream crowd.

Loveland Valley is a different day

The Valley is its own base area with its own chairs and surface lifts. Lift 7 is the main beginner chair, and it was replaced with a new quad in 2025. Lift 3 also serves the Valley. For 2026/27 the resort is adding a beginner surface carpet.

The point of the Valley is that new skiers are not sharing a trail with someone straight-lining from the ridge. Zig-Zag is the longest beginner trail. Once someone is ready for a step up, Tango Road off Lift 2 is a green run with more variety.

A few practical notes for a mixed group:

  • Shuttle: free buses run every 5 to 10 minutes between the Valley and the Basin. The Valley stop is in front of the Valley Sport Shop.
  • Ticket: there is a Valley-only ticket for beginners. Last season Coloradotopia said it came in at less than half a standard one, so check the resort for this year.
  • Timing: last season the Valley opened almost two weeks after the Basin, so do not plan a first-timer day for opening weekend.

Why it stays calmer than the I-70 giants

The resort's own pitch is that its terrain is packed with snow, not people. Take that with a grain of salt, since it is their pitch. Independent sources point the same way. A PeakRankings review said low crowds help keep things moving. Visitors on Tripadvisor describe not waiting at the lift.

The reasons are simple. There is no village to hold people overnight, and nowhere to stay on site, so most people are day-trippers who show up at the same times. Parking is free and close in. The terrain spreads across two base areas, and the Basin has several chairs to pick from once you are off Chet's Dream. The resort also says its focus is an affordable ski day, which is a big reason locals use it.

The calm does have limits. Loveland's parking fills on busy weekends and holidays. PeakRankings also notes that when roads to other resorts close, people pile in here and the lots can fill up. If you want to skip the driving, the resort says the Snowstang bus runs from Denver. Plan to arrive early on any powder weekend, because the first hour is when the Chet's Dream wait is most likely to turn into a real queue.

What to actually do

Loveland opening days get set late, and its best moments are early. A few habits help.

  • Check the resort first: look at skiloveland.com for the day's open lifts and hours. Last season lifts ran 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays and 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekends and holidays.
  • Look before you drive: open the app to see which Loveland lifts are open and the latest Moving, Short, Busy or Long reports, each with how fresh it is. If Chet's Dream says Busy, it points you to the best next chair, and often that means heading for Ptarmigan or Lift 4.
  • Report what you see: checking lifts and reporting waits is free, so a quick tap from the chair helps the next skier decide where to go.

FAQ

Is Loveland open right now?
We cannot confirm it. Our season status is only an estimate. On Nov 5, Unofficial Networks reported that Loveland had no confirmed opening day. Check skiloveland.com for the current status and open lifts.
What is Chair 1 at Loveland?
Chair 1 was a fixed-grip triple built in 1981. In 2018 it was replaced by Chet's Dream, a high-speed quad. Many people still call it Chair 1.
Are Loveland lift lines long?
The resort says its terrain is packed with snow, not people, and visitors often describe short waits. The most likely spot for a wait is Chet's Dream on powder mornings and holidays, since it is the Basin's only high-speed chair. Other chairs are usually an easy move away.
Is parking at Loveland free?
Yes. The resort says all parking is free, with free shuttles every 5 to 10 minutes between the Basin and the Valley. Lots can still fill up on busy weekends and holidays, so arrive early.
Where should beginners go at Loveland?
Loveland Valley. It has its own chairs and surface lifts, with Lift 7 as the main beginner chair. A step-up option is Tango Road off Lift 2.
How do I get to Loveland without driving?
The resort lists the Snowstang bus from Denver for direct service. Check the Snowstang schedule and the resort's site before planning a trip.
