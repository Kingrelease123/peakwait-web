Winter Park Lift Waits: Where the Waits Build, and Where They Don't

Winter Park is the closest big ski area to Denver, so the waits build in predictable places at predictable times. The Mary Jane base, the Gondola and the first hour on a weekend are where most people lose their morning. Here is where the crunch happens, how the drive over Berthoud Pass fits in, and where to go when…

First, is Winter Park open yet?

Start with the boring part. Winter Park's own hours page lists the first day of the 2026-27 season as as soon as possible. A Summit Daily story from October 28 says the resort plans to open as soon as it can. I could not find a published opening date.

The PeakWait app shows a projected opening around November 15. That is our estimate and not a date from the resort. Some sites guessed late October, and nobody has confirmed that either. Check the resort's hours page before you drive anywhere.

When it does open, expect a small slice of the mountain. On opening day in 2024 the resort ran five lifts and about 30 acres, with the Gemini Express, Arrow, Endeavour, Meteor and Spirit. Early season waits come from too many people on too few chairs, not from one bad lift.

Where the waits build at Winter Park and Mary Jane

The Mary Jane base area is the big chokepoint. Reviewers who have skied it point at the Super Gauge Express, the main way out of Mary Jane to the rest of the resort, as the lift that sees serious waits at peak times. Busy mornings are the worst, when everyone who parked on the Mary Jane side loads at once.

Super Gauge is rated at 3,200 skiers per hour, so it moves a lot of people. It still backs up because nearly everyone has to use it.

The second spot is the Panoramic Express, also rated at 3,200 per hour. It is the only lift to the high alpine, and PeakRankings calls it a madhouse on weekends and holidays. If you want the Parsenn Bowl and Cirque side of the mountain, you are sharing one chair with everyone else who wants it.

Then there are the slower chairs. Challenger and Iron Horse are each rated at 1,200 skiers per hour, well under half of Super Gauge. They feed the bumps and trees that Mary Jane is known for, so on a good snow day those waits stretch. One local itinerary says to lap Super Gauge or Iron Horse, whichever is shorter. That is the right instinct.

The gondola and base area crunch

On the Winter Park side, the Gondola is the front door. It replaced the Zephyr Express in 2018 and was tied for the highest capacity gondola in the country when it opened, at 3,600 per hour. Big capacity helps. It does not help much when a whole lodge empties out at 9 a.m.

Right beside it, the Gemini Express and Explorer Express, each rated at 2,400 per hour, lead to Discovery Park and the ski school hub. That zone feels packed around lesson start times. If you are a confident skier, skip it and head for the Gondola or the Olympia Express early.

Timing changed this year. Lifts open an hour earlier on weekends, at 8 a.m., from December 5 through April 4. Weekday hours stay at 9 a.m. The Early Ups add-on, for Ikon and Winter Park pass holders, is now weekdays only and gives an hour of early access on the Gondola and Explorer through the spring.

Parking affects your wait too. The resort says parking reservations are not required and the shuttles are free. G Lot is at the south entrance, the Old Town lots are north on Winter Park Drive, and nobody can park overnight in the Winter Park or Mary Jane lots. The resort says the closest parking to the Gondola is the garage. Walk-up lots fill first on weekends, so arrive early or plan on the shuttle.

The I-70 and Berthoud Pass drive

From Denver you take I-70 west to exit 232, then US-40 over Berthoud Pass. It is roughly 90 minutes in good conditions. The turnoff comes before the Eisenhower Tunnel, so you usually dodge the worst Summit County backup. You do not dodge all of I-70. The stretch between Denver and Empire still jams.

A Denver shuttle operator says Friday afternoons turn into gridlock as the city empties for the weekend. Another local guide suggests leaving Denver by 6:30 a.m. on weekends. That is the real reason people get to the Mary Jane base at the same time. They all left at the same time.

Berthoud Pass can close for avalanche control or bad weather even when I-70 is open. The detour runs west through the Eisenhower Tunnel to Silverthorne, then north on US-40, and adds about 45 miles and 45 to 60 minutes. Check COtrip.org before you leave.

You can skip the drive entirely. The Winter Park Express train is returning in December, and the resort says rides start then. Last season it arrived at the resort at 9:11 a.m. and left at 4:35 p.m. The 2026-27 schedule had not been posted on the pages I read, so check Amtrak. With an 8 a.m. weekend opening, you will miss the first hour if you day-trip by train.

The quieter pods

Winter Park spreads out once you get away from the two bases. These are the spots people mention when the main chairs are slammed.

  • Vasquez Ridge: Intermediate and advanced blues, away from the main hub. It is described as a quieter corner with less traffic.
  • Eagle Wind: Ungroomed glades and the kind of solitude you rarely get here. The chair is rated at 1,800 per hour, so a few people can still make a wait, but it is nothing like Super Gauge.
  • Upper Mary Jane: The Sunnyside Express, rated at 2,400 per hour, serves the upper Mary Jane terrain and made it far more worth lapping. Mellower groomers up there beat the bump crowd.
  • Above the crowd: One guide suggests taking Super Gauge, skiing Bluebell and catching Panoramic to get above the bulk of the people.

One more trick. Eat lunch early at Lunch Rock. The afternoon is when fewer people are exploring the peaks, and the main chairs thin out once the lunch crowd settles in.

What to actually do

  • Pick your side by the clock: Be on the Super Gauge or the Gondola before the first rush, and avoid Mary Jane base mid-morning on weekends. If the Super Gauge wait looks long, try Iron Horse or Challenger, or ski over to Winter Park.
  • Check the lifts before you load: In PeakWait, skiers report each lift as Moving, Short, Busy or Long, with how fresh the report is. The app also shows which lifts are open, so you are not guessing at the Gondola.
  • Plan the drive like a lift choice: Leave Denver early, check COtrip.org for Berthoud Pass, and confirm the opening date on the resort's site. Report the wait you see, because the next skier is staring at the same chair.

FAQ

When does Winter Park open for the 2026-27 season?

The resort's hours page lists the first day as as soon as possible and I could not find a published date. The PeakWait app shows a projected opening around November 15, which is our estimate only. Check the resort's site for the real announcement.

Which Winter Park lift usually has the longest wait?

Reviewers point to the Super Gauge Express out of the Mary Jane base and the Panoramic Express to the high alpine on weekends and holidays. Both are rated at 3,200 skiers per hour in the PeakWait lift list.

Do I need a parking reservation at Winter Park?

The resort says parking reservations are not required and its shuttles are free. Overnight parking is not allowed in the Winter Park or Mary Jane lots.

How long is the drive from Denver to Winter Park?

About 90 minutes in good conditions, via I-70 west to exit 232 and US-40 over Berthoud Pass. Friday afternoons and snowy days run much longer, and the pass can close for avalanche control.

Is there a train to Winter Park?

Yes, the Winter Park Express runs from Denver Union Station and the resort says rides start in December. Check Amtrak for the 2026-27 schedule, since I could not confirm dates.

Is Mary Jane less crowded than Winter Park?

On the slopes it can feel quieter because skiers spread out in the bumps and trees. The base area and the Super Gauge Express are the exception, especially on busy mornings.
