Aspen Snowmass Lift Waits: Where Snowmass Backs Up and When to Bail

One Aspen Snowmass ticket gets you onto four mountains, so a bad wait at Snowmass does not have to ruin your day. The catch is that no lift connects them, so leaving costs you a bus ride. Here is where Snowmass tends to stack up, what changed with the lifts, and when Buttermilk or Highlands is the better call.

Four mountains, one ticket, one catch

A single Aspen Snowmass lift ticket works at Aspen Mountain, Snowmass, Aspen Highlands and Buttermilk, and the resort says there are no blackout dates on tickets. Across the four you get more than 5,700 acres. Snowmass alone is larger than the other three combined, so it is where most people end up.

The catch is that you cannot ski from one mountain to the next. The free Four Mountain Connector bus from RFTA runs in winter only and links all four. The Storm Skiing Journal puts Snowmass 15 to 20 minutes from the other three, depending on which base you want. So bailing is a real decision with a real cost, not a traverse you do on a whim.

What is open when this season

As of today, November 13, nothing at Aspen Snowmass is spinning yet. Snowmass and Aspen Mountain are scheduled to open Thursday, November 26, which is Thanksgiving Day. Aspen Highlands and Buttermilk follow on December 12. Those are the resort's own announced dates, and a local guide notes that dates depend on weather. I found no news of a change, but check the resort's site before you book a flight around them.

That gap matters for this guide. For the first two weeks you only have Snowmass and Aspen Mountain, and Aspen Mountain has no green runs at all according to the Storm Skiing Journal. If you bring a beginner on opening weekend, Buttermilk is not there to bail to yet.

Closing dates run the other way. The resort lists Buttermilk closing April 4, Highlands and Snowmass April 11, and Aspen Mountain April 18. Late in the season your bail options shrink again.

Where Snowmass backs up

Snowmass has two ways out of the Fanny Hill base: the Village Express six-pack and the Elk Camp Gondola. PeakRankings says most visitors start on one of those two. When the gondola was built, the Aspen Times reported it was meant to split the base crowd instead of putting everyone on the Village Express.

  • Village Express: a longtime local on a skiing forum said it gets busy in the morning right after children's ski school starts at 9:30. His rule of thumb was that when it has a 10 minute wait, the gondola is probably 5 or less. That post is old, so treat it as a pattern and not a promise.
  • Elk Camp: the same local called it the most popular chair on the mountain. The Aspen Times reports the 30-year-old quad was replaced with a six-seater that adds 50 percent capacity. The reputation may be older than the lift.
  • Cirque T-Bar: it replaced the old Cirque platter and doubles the capacity, according to the Aspen Times. Expert terrain up high gets easier to lap.
  • Big Burn, Alpine Springs and Elk Camp: the forum names these, along with the Village Express, as the busiest chairs on a normal day.

The one that surprises people is at the end of the day. PeakRankings warns that the Sky Cab, a pulse gondola with limited capacity, can build a long wait when everyone heads back toward the Mall to catch the buses to free parking.

The clock matters more than the lift

To be fair, Snowmass is not a place where a wait runs your whole day. That same forum local said even between Christmas and New Year, lift lines rarely pass 5 minutes and top out around 10. A local lodging guide still calls the stretch around New Year's Eve the busiest two weeks of the year.

So the pattern is simple. Mornings after ski school drops off are the pinch point at the base. Late morning pushes people toward the popular chairs. Around 3 p.m. the village funnels back down.

  • First chair: beat the 9:30 ski school wave and get your first two laps in before it.
  • Lunch: eat early or late. Ullrhof, the mid-mountain restaurant, was slated to reopen this winter after a full rebuild. I would not bet on a quiet lunch there while everyone gets a look.
  • Last run: ski to the Mall if you are taking the bus, not into the village, so the Sky Cab is not your last wait of the day.

When to bail to Buttermilk or Highlands

Do it when the wait is the problem, not the snow. Count on the bus ride on top of any wait you skip, so it pays off best if you decide in the morning and not at 2 p.m.

Buttermilk

Buttermilk is the smallest of the four and has the most green terrain. West Buttermilk is the easy one, and Summit Express, Panda Peak and Tiehack round it out. It is the bail for a mixed group, or when Snowmass base is clogged with ski school. One early-season warning: when Buttermilk opened last December, the Tiehack lift was not running on day one, so check which lifts are turning.

Aspen Highlands

Highlands is the bail for strong skiers. It has five lifts, including Exhibition, Loge Peak, Deep Temerity, Cloud Nine and Thunderbowl, and Skipedia says waits there are essentially unknown. PeakRankings notes Deep Temerity can wait at peak times, and you can traverse out partway to Loge Peak instead. Lifts run 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., so you cannot bail late.

Highland Bowl has no lift. The resort says the hike usually takes about 45 minutes, and the Bowl is usually open from 10 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. Plan three hours if you go.

What to actually do

  • Check before you go: open PeakWait at breakfast and look at the Snowmass lifts. Skiers on the mountain report Moving, Short, Busy or Long, and each lift shows how fresh the report is. If the Village Express and the gondola both read Busy or Long, start at Elk Camp or take the bus. It is free to check lifts and report waits.
  • Use the next-chair pointer: once you are up there, let PeakWait point you to the best next chair, and drop a report when you reach the top. It takes ten seconds and helps the next skier.
  • Decide the bail by the clock: if the base is jammed at 9:30, go to Buttermilk or Highlands before 10. PeakWait Plus ranks where to ski today across nearby resorts by snow and waits, which helps when the question is bigger than one mountain.

FAQ

Does one Aspen Snowmass ticket cover all four mountains?

Yes. The resort says a single lift ticket provides access to Aspen Mountain, Snowmass, Aspen Highlands and Buttermilk. No lift links them, so you ride the free Four Mountain Connector bus between them.

When does Snowmass open for the 2026-27 season?

The resort has announced November 26, 2026 for Snowmass and Aspen Mountain, and December 12 for Aspen Highlands and Buttermilk. Opening depends on weather, so check the resort's site close to your trip.

Which Snowmass lifts get the longest waits?

Historically the Village Express in the morning, plus Elk Camp, Big Burn and Alpine Springs. Elk Camp now has a six-seater with 50 percent more capacity. Late in the day the Sky Cab can back up near the Mall.

How long does it take to get from Snowmass to Highlands or Buttermilk?

Plan on roughly 15 to 20 minutes each way on the free shuttle, plus the walk and the wait for the bus. Highlands and Buttermilk sit close together near Aspen.

Can you ski to the Highland Bowl from a lift?

No. The Bowl is reached by hiking from the top of Loge Peak. The resort says it usually takes about 45 minutes, and a snowcat can save part of the hike.
