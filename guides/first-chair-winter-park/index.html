First Chair at Winter Park: When to Arrive and Where to Start

Winter Park is open for the season, but first chair is not the same time on a Saturday as on a Tuesday. This guide covers when the lifts turn, what early access the resort offers, when to be parked, and the first three lifts to ride before the wait builds. When the lifts start turning

The resort's own hours page lists two schedules this season. From the Nov. 13 opening through Dec. 5, lifts run 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. every day, holidays included. That means today, a Saturday, first chair is at 9, not 8.

Starting Saturday, Dec. 5, the page says lifts open at 8 a.m. on Saturdays and Sundays through April 4, 2027, weather and conditions permitting. The resort frames it as an extra hour for all guests. Weekdays stay at 9. The same page says ticket windows and rental shops open an hour before the lifts.

One wrinkle. The resort's season preview says the earlier weekend hours begin in mid-December or when the Gondola starts running, whichever comes first. That does not read exactly like the Dec. 5 date, so check the hours page the week you go.

Early season also means a smaller mountain. On opening day SnowBrains reported four of 24 lifts open, with Gemini Express and Arrow closed. That was a week ago and more should be turning now. PeakWait shows Winter Park open for the season, but look at the resort's mountain report the morning you drive up to see exactly which chairs are running.

Early lift access the resort sells or includes

There are three things that change who is on a chair before everyone else, and only one is free.

  • Weekend hour: Free. Once the 8 a.m. weekend start begins, the resort says it applies to all guests.
  • Early Ups: A season pass add-on for Winter Park and Ikon pass holders. The resort's Early Ups page says it opens the Gondola and Explorer Express at 8 a.m. Monday through Friday, holidays included. Use starts the day after the Gondola opens, no earlier than Dec. 7, and ends April 2, 2027. A season pass is also required, and it is early access only.
  • Rope Drop Club: Earned, not bought. Eligible pass holders who ski twice between opening day and Nov. 30 get dedicated lanes at Super Gauge and the Gondola on a limited number of days later in the season. See the Rope Drop Club page.

If you are a day skier, you will not be in either of those groups. Two things follow. On weekdays, expect some people already riding the Gondola and Explorer Express before 9, because that is what the add-on is for. And the resort says the Rope Drop lanes merge and alternate with the regular lanes, so the regular side keeps moving.

When to be parked

The resort's parking page says reservations are not required and overnight parking is not allowed in the Winter Park or Mary Jane lots. There is a paid Village Parking Garage in the center of the Village. Free options include shuttle lots, and The Gazette lists the Village Cabriolet Lot, Moffat Lot and Cooper Creek Garage among the free ones.

No reservation also means no guaranteed spot. A local transportation guide says lots can fill before 9 a.m. on weekends and powder days, and that Mary Jane lots tend to fill first on weekends. Here is the schedule I would use.

  • Weekdays and weekends before Dec. 5: Be parked by 8:15 for a 9 a.m. chair, with boots on in the car.
  • Weekends from Dec. 5: Be parked by about 7. The 8 a.m. chair is the whole point, and windows and rental shops open an hour ahead of it.

The resort's Cabriolet shows as open right now, and it is how you get from the outer lots to the Village without a long walk. Leave Denver with more margin than the map says. A snowy Friday morning will cost you the extra hour.

Winter Park base or Mary Jane

For now, this is easy. Mary Jane has no opening date. The resort's hours page says first day of skiing there is as soon as possible. For comparison, last season Mary Jane opened Jan. 9 and closed March 28, according to the resort. Until it opens, start at the Winter Park base. Mary Jane parking lots and the shuttle there open when the Mary Jane base does, per the parking page.

Once Mary Jane is running, pick by what you came for. The Gazette describes the Mary Jane Territory as steep, technical, often ungroomed terrain with moguls and tree runs, including Challenger. If that is your day, park at Mary Jane and start on Super Gauge Express, which sits across from the Mary Jane base ticket office. If you want groomers, a village and the Gondola, park on the Winter Park side.

One caution. Super Gauge and the Gondola are the two lifts with Rope Drop lanes, so they are the two chairs that mean something to the people with the most early-season days. If you see a Super Gauge wait building, you now know why.

The first three lifts to ride

The idea is simple. Ride the big-capacity chairs while the crowd is still deciding where to go. PeakWait lists the Gondola at 3,600 skiers per hour, Panoramic Express at 3,200, and Explorer Express and Gemini Express at 2,400 each. Here is the order I would try on the Winter Park side.

  1. Gondola, or Explorer Express if the Gondola is not running yet. The Gondola moves the most people per hour of anything here. On weekdays these two have the Early Ups crowd, so if you see a bunch of people already loading, start on Gemini Express instead. The resort says it sits right near the base.
  2. Explorer Express. Take the second of the pair as your lap two. If you opened on Gemini Express, this is where you swap over.
  3. Panoramic Express. It climbs to Parsenn Bowl above 12,000 feet, per OnTheSnow. At 3,200 skiers per hour it clears a crowd quickly. Confirm it is open before you commit, because early season does not open everything.

On a Mary Jane day, go Super Gauge Express first, then check the app for which chair has the shortest wait. Do not sit there for four laps because the first one went well.

What to actually do

  • The night before: Check the resort's mountain report for open lifts and the hours page for your day. Pick weekday parking by 8:15, or weekend parking by 7 once Dec. 5 passes.
  • In the car: Open PeakWait to see which Winter Park lifts are open and how fresh each wait report is. It points you to the best next chair, which saves you from guessing at the base.
  • On the snow: When you step off a chair, report the wait as Moving, Short, Busy or Long. It is free, takes a few seconds, and it is how the next skier knows where to go.

FAQ

What time is first chair at Winter Park?

Through Dec. 5, lifts run 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. daily, according to the resort. Starting Dec. 5, the resort lists an 8 a.m. start on Saturdays and Sundays through April 4, 2027, weather and conditions permitting. Weekdays stay at 9. Check the hours page, since the resort's season preview words the start of the weekend hours a little differently.

Do I need a parking reservation at Winter Park?

No. The resort says parking reservations are not required, and overnight parking is not allowed in its lots. That also means spots are not held for you, so arrive early on weekends. The Winter Park Express train from Denver Union Station has also run on weekends in past seasons. Check Amtrak and the resort for this season's schedule.

Can I buy early access to the lifts?

The resort sells Early Ups as a season pass add-on. It opens the Gondola and Explorer Express at 8 a.m. Monday through Friday, starting the day after the Gondola opens and no earlier than Dec. 7. It requires a season pass. Weekends get the 8 a.m. start for everyone.

Is Mary Jane open?

The resort's hours page says Mary Jane opens as soon as possible and gives no date. Last season it opened Jan. 9. Check the resort's mountain report before driving up for Mary Jane specifically.

What if Winter Park looks too crowded?

PeakWait Plus ranks where to ski today across nearby resorts by snow and waits. The app currently shows Arapahoe Basin, Copper Mountain and Loveland as open for the season, confirmed by the resort's lift feed or skiers on the mountain. Check each resort's own site before you change plans.
