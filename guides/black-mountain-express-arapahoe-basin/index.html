Black Mountain Express at Arapahoe Basin: Beating the Base Wait and Lift Waits

Black Mountain Express is the one chair at Arapahoe Basin that almost everyone rides before doing anything else. This season it was the only lift running on opening day, and the wait at the base showed it. Here is what the chair serves, why the wait builds, when it thins out, and where to go once you are up top.

What Black Mountain Express actually serves

Black Mountain Express is a high-speed quad built by Leitner in 2010. It replaced the old Exhibition lift, and A-Basin's first detachable chair is how one local lodging guide describes it. The ride is about 876 meters long and climbs roughly 219 meters. It takes a bit over three minutes, and the chair is rated for about 2,000 people an hour.

It drops you at Black Mountain Lodge, mid-mountain at 11,500 feet according to the resort. That is also the base of the Lenawee Express. Almost every green and most of the blues on the front side hang off this one chair, plus a few black runs. One local ski guide puts the beginner terrain, apart from the learning area, all on this lift.

So when you ask what Black Mountain Express serves, the honest answer is most of the mountain you can reach without a hike.

Why the wait at the base builds

The math is simple. The chair moves about 2,000 people an hour, and A-Basin skiers do not arrive evenly. They arrive when the lifts open. Weekday lifts start at 9 a.m. and weekend lifts at 8:30 a.m., per the resort's operating hours, and everyone who parked on the early side is standing there at once.

Three things stack on top of that.

  • One way up: The base area has Black Mountain Express plus the beginner lifts. Everything else on the front side starts from the top of this chair.
  • Close parking: The lots sit right above the base. On days when reservations apply, the resort says its lots typically fill by midmorning. A full lot means a full base.
  • Families: The chair sits next to Molly Hogan and the carpets, so first-timers and their groups pile into the same spot.

For a sense of scale, one reviewer on a past opening day described the group wait as chaotic-looking but about 15 minutes a run. That is one person's day, not a promise, but it is the right ballpark for a crowded morning.

Early season: when it is the only way up

A-Basin opened for the 2026-27 season on October 28 at 11 a.m. Black Mountain Express was the only lift spinning, and High Noon was the only run, according to SKI and GearJunkie. That is the early-season deal here. If the chair is not turning, nobody is skiing.

It stayed that way for weeks. The Denver Gazette reported that until Lenawee Express opened on November 20, Black Mountain Express was the only operating lift on the mountain. With one chair and one run, every skier in the building shares the same wait, and there is nowhere to dodge it. Going up to a different lift is not an option, so your only moves are timing and patience.

It is loosening now. Lenawee Express opened on November 20, and the resort's own blog said Molly Hogan and the Pika conveyor would open on Wednesday, November 25. Pallavicini is the one to watch. Last season it did not open until January 10, so do not assume it is running. Check the resort's site at arapahoebasin.com for what is spinning today.

One bit of good news. The resort says parking reservations are not needed for opening day or the early season. Weekend reservations start January 2, 2027.

When the wait is shortest

Weekdays win. Parking is free Monday through Friday without a reservation, but it is first come, first served, so a quiet Tuesday still beats a Saturday by a lot. On weekends, the reservation window runs from January 2 through May 2, 2027, and it covers 6 a.m. to 1 p.m. That caps the number of cars, which helps the base.

Within a day, here is what regulars usually do.

  • Be there before the first chair: If you are on the snow when the lift starts, you ride before the crowd stacks up. On weekends that is 8:30 a.m.
  • Skip the midmorning surge: The lots fill by then, and the wait is usually at its worst.
  • Try the last hour: Black Mountain Express runs until 4 p.m. Plenty of skiers have gone home by then, though snow and weather can change that.

Treat all of that as a pattern, not a rule. Conditions swing, and a fresh report beats a guess.

Which chair to move to once you are up the hill

The best way to beat the Black Mountain Express wait is to ride it less. Once you are at mid-mountain, stay up.

  • Lenawee Express: A six-pack that went in around 2022. Since November 20 it has served a top-to-bottom route of Lenawee Face, Dercum's Gulch and High Noon, all blue. Lap this instead of dropping to the base. A local guide notes that runs off the East Wall land at the base of Lenawee, so you can keep going without returning to the base wait.
  • Pallavicini Lift: The steep stuff on the front side. It is the big prize when it opens, so expect crowds when it does. Last season it opened on a Saturday around noon and drew a swarm.
  • Zuma Lift: Blue, black and double black terrain in Montezuma Bowl, on the backside. Check that it is open before you traverse over.
  • Beavers: Hike-to and gated terrain for strong skiers. Not a place to bail out of a wait.
  • Molly Hogan Lift and Pika Place Carpet: The beginner area at the base. If you are teaching someone, skip Black Mountain Express and start here.

Mid-mountain gets crowded around the top of Black Mountain Express and the base of Lenawee, so the next wait you hit may be a short one in a new place. That still beats starting over at the base.

FAQ

Is Black Mountain Express the only lift open at Arapahoe Basin?

Not anymore. It was the only operating lift from opening day on October 28 until Lenawee Express opened on November 20. The resort's blog also said Molly Hogan and the Pika conveyor would open November 25. PeakWait shows which lifts are open, and the resort's site is the final word, especially for Pallavicini, Zuma and Beavers.

How long is the Black Mountain Express ride?

About three minutes. The lift is roughly 876 meters long and rated for about 2,000 people an hour, so the ride is quick and the wait at the base is where the time goes.

What time does Black Mountain Express open?

The resort lists 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Monday through Friday, and 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Saturdays, Sundays and select holidays. Hours can change, so check the resort's site on the day.

Do I need a parking reservation at A-Basin?

Not in the early season. Weekend reservations are required from January 2 through May 2, 2027, and weekday parking is free without one. Book through the resort's parking site and check the rules before you go.

Can beginners avoid the Black Mountain Express wait?

Partly. The Molly Hogan Lift and Pika Place Carpet serve the learning area at the base, so first-timers can stay there. Once they want greens beyond that area, Black Mountain Express is the way up.
