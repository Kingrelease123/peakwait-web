Big Sky Lift Waits: Big Terrain, Short Waits, One Long Tram

Big Sky lists 5,850 skiable acres and a lift system built to move people, so most chairs here barely make you slow down. The exception is the Lone Peak Tram, where the wait is the whole story. Here is how Swift Current 6, Ramcharger 8 and the new Explorer Gondola fit together, and how to plan your tram laps.

Big terrain, thin crowds

Big Sky lists 5,850 skiable acres and 4,350 vertical feet, topping out at the 11,166 foot summit of Lone Peak. National Geographic calls it the third-largest ski resort in America by skiable terrain, and says decades of planning help it still feel uncrowded. You notice that by about 10 a.m. Everybody who looked like a crowd at the base has simply vanished into the trees.

PeakWait tracks 45 lifts here, from magic carpets to a tram. With that many options, lift lines tend to spread out instead of stacking up on one chair. The one place that breaks the pattern is the single lift everybody wants for the same reason. We will get to it.

Swift Current 6 and Ramcharger 8: the workhorses

Two big lifts leave the Mountain Village area, and both are worth knowing by name. Ramcharger 8 went in during 2018 and was the first eight-person chairlift in North America. It runs about 4,578 feet in roughly 4.5 minutes, and the app lists it at 3,600 skiers per hour. It heads toward Andesite Mountain.

Swift Current 6 came online for the 2021-22 season and replaced an older high-speed quad. The resort says it is 8,555 feet long and takes about 7 minutes, and the app lists it at 3,000 skiers per hour. It is the main artery toward Lone Peak, so it is the one you will ride most if you want the steep stuff. Both have heated seats and bubbles, which matters more at 8 a.m. in January than any spec sheet.

  • Ramcharger 8: Your easy first lap, and the better pick if Swift Current looks backed up.
  • Swift Current 6: The long ride up toward Lone Peak. Use the seven minutes to pick your next run.

The Lone Peak Tram wait

Here is the catch. The app lists the Lone Peak Tram at 850 skiers per hour. Swift Current 6 is listed at 3,000. So the tram moves less than a third of the people per hour, and all of them want the same few couloirs. A new tram debuted in December 2023, carries about 75 people per trip according to local guides, and the resort says the ride is just under four minutes. Even so, it is the bottleneck.

The resort has been open about this. It took the tram out of lift tickets in 2021-22 to keep average waits under 30 minutes. Then starting in 2024-25 it put the tram back into all lift tickets and said it would keep managing how many riders go up per trip. One local guide says weekend tram waits run 30 to 60 minutes and midweek is dramatically better. Treat those as what people say, not a promise.

Wind is the other wild card. The summit is exposed, and one local guide says the tram routinely does not run on windy days. Have a backup plan before you head to the Bowl.

If you are on an Ikon Pass, the resort says Ikon holders use Autocharge to ride the tram. Set that up on the couch, not at the tram dock. Check the resort's site for your exact pass, because rules have changed more than once.

The Explorer Gondola changes the route to the tram

The Explorer Gondola opened December 20, 2025, so this is its first full winter. It is a 10-person gondola that connects Mountain Village to the Bowl and links straight to the base of the Lone Peak Tram. It replaced a fixed-grip double chair that dated back to the resort's 1973 opening. The app lists both gondola sections at 2,800 skiers per hour.

That gives you two ways to the tram. You can ride the gondola up from the village, or you can ride Powder Seeker 6, which the app lists at 1,800 skiers per hour. One itinerary says to exit Powder Seeker to the right and you will see the tram base on your left. Both routes end in the same place, so the tram wait is where they meet.

The gondola also lets non-skiers reach the summit by transferring to the tram, so you will see boots mixed in with sneakers. The resort says the new Kircliff observatory sits at the top. That is one more reason the tram can feel busy on a clear day.

Where to go when the tram wait is long

Big Sky is a good place to walk away from a wait. The Avant Ski suggests skipping the main base lifts on busy days and trying Challenger, Lone Tree, Thunder Wolf, Shedhorn and Dakota, plus the Moonlight Basin side. Dakota is on the south side of Lone Peak. Madison 8 is a big, fast chair on the Moonlight side, and the app lists it at 2,800 skiers per hour.

A decent plan is to ride Swift Current early, take one tram lap if the wait is short, and then spend the middle of the day on the chairs that do not funnel into a single exit. Come back to the tram late in the afternoon if you still have legs. Late days sometimes let you lap it, but I would not count on it.

When Big Sky opens, and what to check first

The resort has announced opening day for Wednesday, November 25, 2026, with lifts running 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. from Mountain Village. Daily operations are listed to end April 11, 2027. As of today, November 16, the resort is not open yet, so check bigskyresort.com for what is actually spinning before you book anything around the tram.

Do not expect every lift on opening day, and do not expect the tram to be running. Resorts add terrain as snow builds up. The resort's own conditions page is the source of truth for what is open.

What to actually do

  • Go early and calm: Head for the tram first thing on a clear, low-wind weekday. Skip it on a windy Saturday and save the couloirs for another day.
  • Check the tram before you commit: In PeakWait, look at the latest Moving, Short, Busy or Long report on the tram and how fresh it is. If it says Long, head to Challenger, Lone Tree or the Moonlight side and look again after lunch.
  • Report what you see: When you ride the tram or Swift Current, tap in the wait. It takes five seconds, and it helps the next skier standing at the base deciding where to go. Ikon holders should also set up Autocharge before leaving the house.

FAQ

Are Big Sky lift waits long?

Mostly no. With 5,850 acres and a modern lift system, most chairs move quickly. The Lone Peak Tram is the exception, because it carries far fewer people per hour than the chairs that feed it.

How long is the Lone Peak Tram wait?

One local guide says 30 to 60 minutes on weekends, and much shorter midweek. The resort has said it aims to keep average tram waits under 30 minutes. Wind can close the tram, so check the resort's site for today's status.

When does Big Sky open for 2026-27?

The resort has announced Wednesday, November 25, 2026, with lifts running 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Check bigskyresort.com for which lifts are running, since not everything opens on day one.

Is the Lone Peak Tram included with a lift ticket?

The resort said that starting in 2024-25 all lift tickets include the tram. Ikon holders have used Autocharge to ride it, so check the resort's site for the rules on your pass.

Which lifts out of Mountain Village carry the most skiers?

By the capacities listed in the app, Ramcharger 8 is highest at 3,600 skiers per hour, then Swift Current 6 at 3,000, then the Explorer Gondola at 2,800. Ramcharger is a good alternative when Swift Current has a crowd.
