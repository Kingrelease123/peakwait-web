Super Bee at Copper Mountain: The Wait and How to Ski Around It

Super Bee is the six-pack that most people at Copper Mountain aim for first, so it collects a crowd. It serves the east side of the mountain, where the runs get steeper and longer. This guide covers what it reaches, when the wait tends to ease, and which other chairs drop you in the same place or somewhere quieter.

What Super Bee actually serves

Super Bee is a six-person detachable chair that loads in East Village, next to Copper Station. It opened in 1998, and Lift Blog calls it Copper's first six pack. Copper's own trail map lists about 2,300 vertical feet and an eight minute ride. Skiresort.info says about nine. Either way, it is a long ride for one chair.

It is rated at 2,900 skiers per hour. Only American Eagle, American Flyer and Timberline Express are rated higher at Copper. That is a lot of chair, and it still fills up.

From the top, Snowpak says to bear left and then right for Far East or Too Much. Both face east, so you get morning sun. Super Bee also feeds long runs that run alongside the Resolution lift down toward Spaulding Bowl. Glades like Black Bear and Freefall sit between Super Bee and Alpine. One guide describes the lift as climbing to Resolution Bowl.

Why so many skiers start there

It is easy to get to. The Alpine and Far East lots are free, and the Black Route bus runs from Alpine to East Village and stops in front of Copper Station. The Purple Route does the same from Far East. Copper says the Alpine lot is also about a seven minute walk from East Village.

You park for free, ride a bus or walk a few minutes, and the biggest chair on the east side is right there. Super Bee BBQ is at the base too, so you can think about lunch at 9 a.m.

It also has a reputation. Snowpak says stronger skiers can escape the crowds by starting on Super Bee, especially on weekends. Another guide calls East Village the least crowded base. Plenty of people read the same advice and show up at the same chair. When that happens, your own eyes at the base beat any guide.

When the wait is shortest

I can't give you a magic minute, and I won't invent one. Here is what skiers and guides commonly say.

  • Weekdays: One older guide says the lift lines are not a problem midweek, but can run up to 12 minutes on high-season weekends.
  • Lunch: Charter Sports lists the lunch hours as a good time to dodge the longest waits.
  • Late afternoon: The same source recommends late afternoon, when many people have quit.
  • Mid-morning, from another direction: MountainMap suggests starting in Center Village, lapping American Flyer early, then moving east to Super Bee once things thin out.

Copper opens at 9 a.m., according to one local guide. The first hour is still a gamble at Super Bee, because that is exactly when everyone with the same idea shows up. If the wait looks Busy or Long at the base, don't stand in it. Take one of the options below.

Same terrain, different chair

You can reach the top of Super Bee without riding it.

  • Excelerator: It tops out near the top of Super Bee and Resolution, per Snowpak. It is rated 2,450 skiers per hour. If you start in Center Village, American Eagle drops you right by its base.
  • Resolution: It also reaches the same upper area and runs next to the long Spaulding Bowl runs. It is rated 1,800 skiers per hour, so a crowd can back it up faster than Super Bee.
  • Alpine: It is rated 1,200 skiers per hour. Snowpak suggests it for laps on Far East and Too Much, and for the glades between Alpine and Super Bee.

Going the other way, you can ski to the Super Bee base from Center Village. From the top of American Flyer, Snowpak says to turn left onto Coppertone, then cut right onto Upper Skid Road and follow Skid Road to East Village. Copper also points skiers from the American Eagle top down Skid Road to East Village.

From the tops of Resolution, Super Bee and Excelerator, you can also take the intermediate Ptarmigan over to Solitude Station at the top of American Eagle.

Quieter terrain when the base looks bad

Charter Sports names Timberline Express and the newer Three Bears as good ways to dodge congestion while still covering ground. Timberline Express is rated 3,200 skiers per hour, so it can swallow a crowd. Three Bears is rated 1,200.

Summit Daily reported that Three Bears opens trails like Boulderado, Buffalo Stampede and Denverite. Mountain Chief and Blackjack open Copper Bowl terrain like Matchless, Six Shooter and Otto Bahn. Both of those are also rated 1,200 skiers per hour. They are small chairs, so one big group can change the wait. Watch for that.

One local guide also says Spaulding Bowl sees less traffic than Resolution Bowl, and that West Village empties out after morning lessons end. Mellow afternoon laps on the west side can beat a Busy Super Bee.

Right now and race weekend

In PeakWait, Copper Mountain shows as open for the season, with lifts closed for the night. Skiers and the resort's lift feed have confirmed it is running. That does not mean every lift is spinning. Copper opened on November 8 with American Eagle terrain, including Rhapsody, Fair Play and Main Vein, and no beginner terrain at first. The resort said it would add lifts as conditions allow.

So before you commit to East Village, check that Super Bee is actually open today on Copper's site at coppercolorado.com.

The Stifel Cup is scheduled for November 28 and 29. Copper's officials say it features men's super-G on Saturday and giant slalom on Sunday, on Rosi's Run, with spectator viewing in East Village. That is also Thanksgiving weekend, so expect East Village to be busier. Check Copper's site for how the race affects lifts and parking.

What to actually do

  • Check first: Open PeakWait before you leave the car. It shows which Copper lifts are open and how fresh each wait report is. If Super Bee shows Busy or Long, it points you to the best next chair.
  • Park free and pick your start: Use the Alpine lot and the Black Route bus or walk to East Village on a quiet day. On a busy one, ride American Eagle from Center Village, then Excelerator, and reach the same top without the Super Bee base.
  • Report what you see: When you ride a chair, tap Moving, Short, Busy or Long in the app. It is free, and it helps the next skier decide where to go.

FAQ

How long is the Super Bee wait at Copper Mountain?

It changes through the day. One older guide says the lift lines are fine on weekdays and can reach about 12 minutes on high-season weekends. Check the latest skier reports before you commit to the base.

Where is the Super Bee lift and where does it go?

It loads in East Village at Copper Station. It climbs the east side toward Resolution Bowl, with runs like Far East and Too Much, and long runs toward Spaulding Bowl.

Where do I park for Super Bee?

The Alpine and Far East lots are free. Copper says the Alpine lot is about a seven minute walk to East Village. The Black Route bus runs from Alpine and the Purple Route runs from Far East.

Can I reach the top of Super Bee without riding it?

Yes. Excelerator tops out near the top of Super Bee and Resolution, and American Eagle from Center Village drops you at its base. Resolution also reaches the same upper area.

Is Super Bee being replaced?

Lift Blog covered a Copper master plan that proposed replacing it with an eight-place lift. That was a proposal. Check Copper's site for anything built or scheduled.
