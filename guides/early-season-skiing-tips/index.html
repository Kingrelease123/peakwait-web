Early Season Skiing Tips: How to Beat the Wait When Only a Few Lifts Are Open

In November, a ski day can mean one chair, one run and everyone you know standing at the bottom of it. Here is what is actually open in Colorado right now, why the first weekend after any opening is the worst for waits, and how to still get a good day out of a ribbon of white.

What November skiing looks like in Colorado right now

As of November 3, three Colorado areas have lifts running for the season, and none of them is running much. Arapahoe Basin opened on October 28 with a single lift, the Black Mountain Express, which serves one intermediate run called High Noon. That was the whole mountain on day one of its 80th season.

Keystone opened on October 30. You ride the River Run Gondola up and take laps on Montezuma Express, skiing Schoolmarm and Silver Spoon, a little over two miles of snow in total. Both runs are green. Wolf Creek opened on Halloween with three lifts, including Bonanza and Nova, which covers roughly 30 percent of the mountain.

Wolf Creek comes with a catch. Its own announcement says it is closed Wednesday and Thursday, November 4 and 5, then spins Friday through Sunday until there is significantly more snow. The app may show it as open for the season, which is true, but it will not help you if you drive up on a Wednesday. Check the resort's site before you go.

More is coming. Breckenridge and Copper both announced November 6, and Vail and Eldora announced November 13. Winter Park, Steamboat and Loveland show dates in the app that are only our estimates, with nothing from the resort behind them. Treat those as closed until the resort says otherwise.

Why the wait is worse on opening weekends

It is simple arithmetic with a little bad luck on top. Every skier who has been waiting since spring shows up in the same few days, and they all have to squeeze through the same one or two loading stations. With only a handful of runs open, nobody can spread out. As one early-season guide puts it, everyone funnels onto the same few runs.

Then add the party. A-Basin put live music in the base area and giveaways in the lift lines on opening day, and people came for that as much as for the turns. At Keystone, a local TV report from opening Friday described a long wait for the first chair of the season.

The day is also short. Keystone ran 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. and Wolf Creek runs 9 to 4. Everyone wants first chair, everyone wants lunch at noon, and nobody wants to be the one who missed the last lap. So the wait comes in waves, and the waves are bigger than you expect from a tiny mountain.

Wolf Creek's Friday through Sunday schedule makes this worse. Skiers who might have spread across a week now have three days to choose from. If a resort is open only on weekends, the weekend is the crowd.

Pick the right day and the right hour

The cheapest trick is a weekday. Breckenridge opens on a Friday, November 6. The weekend that follows is the one to skip if you can. Go Monday or Tuesday of the following week, or wait until the second weekend, when the first rush has burned off.

If you have to go on a busy day, get there early. One peak-times guide suggests arriving about 30 minutes before lifts open, and says the first two hours and last two hours of a ski day tend to have the shortest waits. In November that last stretch is real. Skip the afternoon coffee and take your final laps while everyone else is in the lodge.

Parking is one thing you can stop worrying about at A-Basin. The resort says reservations are not required for opening day or the early season, and it plans to require them only on Saturdays and Sundays from January 2 to May 2, 2027. No reservation also means no cap, so the early arrival is on you.

Skiing a ribbon of white without wrecking your season

The snow is thin and the ground is not far underneath. Wolf Creek's own snow report tells you to expect rocks and unmarked obstacles. That is not a throwaway line.

  • Bring old skis. One ski site says early November is no time for new sticks, because rocks are part of the deal.
  • Stay on the open runs. Another guide says off-piste terrain in early season often hides obstacles that will not be covered until well into winter.
  • Ski slower than you think you need to. Low-angle runs are safer on a thin base anyway, and you will be sharing them with people who have not skied since April.
  • Do not skin up. Keystone says uphill access is closed in the early season, and once it opens, only designated routes are allowed before and after lift hours.

A ribbon of snow is also a narrow place to be. Stop at the edge, not in the middle, and look uphill before you start again. The first weekend has plenty of people on rusty legs.

How to handle the wait when there is only one chair

With one lift, you cannot ride a different chair to dodge a long wait. You can only change when you ride it. Here is what works for me.

Ski your first lap right away, then stop and look at the loading area before you commit to a second. If the wait is moving, keep lapping. If it has backed up, take your break now, grab food or switch to a short warm-up in the base area, and come back in 20 minutes. Everyone else is thinking about lunch at the same time, so go a little earlier or a little later than noon.

At Keystone you have a gondola and a chair in play, so check which one is backing up before you pick a spot. When the wait is bad, do not take it out on the person at the front of it. They are just trying to ski.

What to actually do this week

Here is the short version for your next early-season day.

  • Check what is running first. Open PeakWait to see which lifts are open and how fresh the waits are, then confirm on the resort's own site, because early-season schedules change. Wolf Creek's Wednesday and Thursday closure is the example.
  • Go on a weekday, or be early. Aim to be parked 30 minutes before the lifts open, and plan your last laps for the final two hours of the day.

FAQ

Is it worth skiing in November when only one or two lifts are open?

Yes, if you go in with the right expectations. You get a few easy runs, thin snow and a short day, but you also get the first turns of the season, usually at a lower pace. Go on a weekday and bring old skis.

Which Colorado ski areas are open right now?

As of November 3, Arapahoe Basin, Keystone and Wolf Creek have opened for the season. Wolf Creek says it is closed November 4 and 5 and then runs Friday through Sunday until more snow arrives, so check the resort's site first. Breckenridge and Copper have announced November 6.

Why are lift lines so long on opening weekend?

Very few lifts and runs are open, so everyone shares the same loading station. Add opening-day events and a short operating day, and the wait builds in waves around first chair and lunch. A weekday is usually calmer.

Do I need a parking reservation at Arapahoe Basin in early season?

Not on opening day or in the early season. Arapahoe Basin says it plans to require reservations only on Saturdays and Sundays from January 2 to May 2, 2027. Plan to arrive early anyway.

What gear should I bring for early season skiing?

Old skis or a board you do not mind scratching, since rocks and unmarked obstacles are common. Dress warm for a cold, short day, and stay on open, marked runs. Ski a bit slower than you would in January.
