Winter Park vs Copper Mountain: Which to Ski on a Saturday

Winter Park and Copper Mountain are both on the Ikon Pass and both a morning drive from Denver. On a Saturday they are very different days. One sends you over Berthoud Pass and the other sends you up I-70 with everyone else. Here is how they compare on the drive, the waits, the terrain and the parking.

Where each mountain stands right now

Check this first, because early season changes the answer. Copper Mountain is open for the season, and PeakWait shows its lifts running today, confirmed by the resort's lift feed or skiers on the mountain. It was not smooth. Summit Daily reported that warm overnight temperatures pushed Copper's Nov. 6 opening to Sunday, Nov. 8, and that the resort planned to add terrain as conditions allow. Expect a short list of open runs, which means everyone is stacked onto the same few chairs.

Winter Park is a different story. When I read the resort's hours page, it said the first day of the season had not been set and that it would open as soon as possible. Our own estimate puts it around Nov. 15. That is an estimate and not a date from the resort, so look at winterparkresort.com before you load the car.

If you are planning a Saturday later this month, note that Copper hosts the Stifel Copper Cup on Nov. 28 and 29, with the men's Super-G on that Saturday and a free concert in Center Village that night. Race weekends bring a crowd to the base.

The drive from the Front Range

Winter Park is about 67 miles from Denver. You take I-70 to exit 232, then US-40 north over Berthoud Pass, and you get there in about 90 minutes when conditions are good. The big plus is that you leave the interstate before the Eisenhower Tunnel, so you skip the worst of the Summit County crawl.

Copper is about 75 miles from Denver at I-70 exit 195. It is the more direct drive on paper. On a Saturday it is the one with the problem. One driving guide says weekend I-70 traffic typically adds 60 to 90 minutes westbound on Saturday and Sunday mornings. Leave at dawn or accept that you are part of the parade.

Winter Park has its own risk. Berthoud Pass closes now and then in winter for avalanche control. If it does, the detour runs through the Eisenhower Tunnel and CO-9, about 15 miles longer. Colorado's traction law applies on both roads during winter storms, which in practice means AWD with snow-rated tires or chains.

One more option for Winter Park. The Winter Park Express ski train returns in December, run with the Colorado Department of Transportation. It skips the pass entirely.

Waits and where they build

Neither mountain has a secret chair that never gets a wait on a Saturday. What you can do is know the pattern.

At Copper, Center Village is the main entrance, and the American Eagle chondola and American Flyer are the lifts most people walk to first. If you are an advanced skier, a local shop's tip is to start on Super Bee in East Village on weekends. Then head for Resolution and Alpine, the older double chairs on the east edge that serve long mogul runs. Doubles load slowly but they rarely draw a crowd.

At Winter Park, the weekend helps you. Starting this winter, lifts open at 8:00 a.m. on Saturdays and Sundays, an hour earlier than the weekday start, according to the resort. Be on a chair at 8:00 and you can get a few laps in before the late arrivals work through the base.

Once you are on snow, waits shift by the hour. Skiers on the mountain report them in the app as Moving, Short, Busy or Long on each lift, with how fresh the report is. A Long on one chair and Moving on the next one over is your cue to traverse.

Terrain: sprawl versus sorted

Winter Park has 3,081 acres split into seven territories. The Winter Park side is long groomed cruising and family terrain. Mary Jane next door is known for bumps and trees. Parsenn Bowl is open, above-treeline skiing reached from Mary Jane by the Panoramic Express. Vasquez Ridge is often quieter. It is a place to roam all day without repeating yourself.

Copper has roughly 2,500 acres, and its trick is that the mountain sorts you. Beginners go west to Union Creek and the West Village. Intermediates stay central off American Eagle, American Flyer and Timberline. Advanced skiers go east off Super Bee. About half the terrain is rated advanced or expert, and about a quarter is beginner. A mixed group can split up in the morning and meet for lunch without anyone being lost.

  • Pick Winter Park if your group wants variety, bumps or open bowl skiing.
  • Pick Copper if your group spans beginner to expert and you want to keep everyone sorted.

Early in the season, both are running on whatever snow has been made, so expect fewer runs than the trail maps show.

Parking: free is not the same as easy

Winter Park does not require parking reservations, and its parking is free. The North Bench lot is walk-in, walk-out, with a pedestrian bridge to the base. The G Lot shuttle runs from Nov. 14 through April 4, from 7:40 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. All resort shuttles are free. No overnight parking in the resort or Mary Jane lots.

Copper keeps its free parking farther from the lifts. The Alpine and Far East lots are free seven days a week and connect to the villages by shuttle. The interior lots, Ten Mile, Chapel, Union Creek, Beeler and Wheeler, are paid in winter, and they become free after 2 p.m. Copper's season parking passes are sold out, and pass holders get exclusive use of the Flyer lot. A local rental shop warns that weekend lots fill early.

So at Winter Park you can pull in and park for free. At Copper you have a decision to make: walk and shuttle from the free lots, or pay to park close. If you show up after the main push, you may be sent to the farther lot.

What to actually do on Saturday

My call: Winter Park for terrain, Copper for a group of mixed levels. Both can work, and the right choice depends on the snow, the roads and which lifts are running.

  • Leave early: Be on the road before dawn for Copper, and aim to be on a Winter Park chair at the 8:00 a.m. weekend start once the resort has opened. Look at the resort's site the night before for lift status and any Berthoud Pass closure.
  • Park free first: At Copper, take Alpine or Far East and the shuttle. At Winter Park, go straight to North Bench or the G Lot shuttle.

FAQ

Is Winter Park open yet?

Not as far as we can confirm. When I read the resort's hours page, it said the first day had not been set and that it would open as soon as possible. Our own estimate is around Nov. 15, but that is an estimate and not a resort date. Check winterparkresort.com before you drive.

Is Copper Mountain open?

The app shows Copper open for the season, with lifts confirmed running today. Summit Daily reported that the resort pushed its opening from Nov. 6 to Nov. 8 because of warm weather. Terrain was limited at first, so check the resort's site for what is open.

Which is the easier drive from Denver on a Saturday?

Winter Park is about 67 miles and leaves I-70 before the Eisenhower Tunnel, but it crosses Berthoud Pass. Copper is about 75 miles straight up I-70, where weekend mornings typically add 60 to 90 minutes westbound. Winter Park is the safer bet for traffic, and Copper is the safer bet if the pass is closed or icy.

Is parking free at both?

Winter Park's resort parking is free and needs no reservation. Copper has free parking in the Alpine and Far East lots, with paid interior lots that go free after 2 p.m. Both are subject to change, so look at the resort's parking page.

Are both on the Ikon Pass?

Yes. Copper's own site lists the Ikon Pass, and Winter Park lists it as its lift pass too.

Does Winter Park open earlier on weekends?

Yes. The resort says lifts open at 8:00 a.m. on Saturdays and Sundays this winter, an hour earlier than the weekday start.
