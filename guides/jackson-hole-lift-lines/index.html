The tram wait: why Big Red moves slower than you want

The Aerial Tram, nicknamed Big Red, is the lift everyone at Teton Village is thinking about when they clip in. It is also rated at 650 skiers per hour. The Bridger Gondola next door is rated at 2,400, and the Sublette Quad Chair is rated at 2,200. So the lift with the biggest reputation moves roughly a quarter of what the gondola does. That is how you end up with a crowd.

Back of the napkin math: at 650 an hour, 300 people ahead of you is a little under half an hour of standing around. One ski guide site says you should expect plenty of people waiting first thing in the morning, right after lunch and anytime on a powder day. It also says local die-hards show up around 6:00am to get first box.

Tram habits that keep you out of trouble

  • Skip the obvious times: First thing and right after lunch are both known for crowds, so a mid-morning ride is worth a try.
  • Hold your spot honestly: Local writers say not to lean your skis on the railing and wander off for coffee and a bathroom break. Twenty minutes later, nobody has saved you anything.
  • Bring breakfast: A Jackson Hole lodging blog says to show up early on a powder day with coffee and a breakfast burrito. Be ready to wait, and it says the tram wait moves faster than it looks.

The tram serves the vast, challenging terrain that sets Jackson Hole apart from most resorts. Whether it is worth the wait is your call, and on a powder day plenty of people decide it is not.

Bridger Gondola and Sweetwater: the other front doors

The Bridger Gondola is rated at 2,400 skiers per hour, which makes it the highest capacity lift on the list. Both high speed gondolas, Bridger and Sweetwater, lead to acres of moderate groomed terrain for intermediates, which is a different world from what the tram serves. A local guide adds that plenty of blues and black diamonds are reachable from the gondola too.

Sweetwater is the one people forget. It runs in two stages, and both the Lower and Upper gondolas are rated at 2,000 skiers per hour. The mid-station is Solitude Station, home to Trappers, which serves lunch and apres.

Because Sweetwater is two stages, a short wait at the bottom does not tell you the whole story. Check the upper gondola too before you commit.

The powder day scramble at Teton Village

A powder morning at the Village has its own weather system, and it is made of people. A local guide says most people at the base are programmed to head for the tram or the gondola. His advice is to let them. One blogger even photographed a Sunday in January 2014 when the tram wait and the gondola wait ran into each other.

Here is a plan that does not involve standing still:

  1. Decide at breakfast whether you are doing the tram. If yes, get there early and bring snacks. If no, skip to the next step.
  2. Take the Bridger Gondola and go off trail. A Jackson Hole guide says powder stashes on the lower mountain hold up longer because so many people rush to the upper mountain.
  3. Try Apres Vous, a high speed quad rated at 2,000 skiers per hour. Most people there want groomers. The guide points to Saratoga Bowl, a set of black runs reached only from Apres Vous, as a powder spot.
  4. Ride the Thunder Quad Chair. A review site names Paint Brush, Tower Three Chute and Hoops Gap as top places to find a stash.

If you do get high, the same review site picks the Hobacks for deep powder, and says to get there early because the sun hits that part of the mountain first. Warm days turn it to sludge.

Parking and getting to the lifts without losing an hour

Plenty of waits begin in the parking lot. Teton Village lots charge a fee during the day, though carpools of four or more in the Ranch Lot park free, as do hotel guests and short-term or ADA parking. Parking is free after 1:00pm in the Ranch Lot and after 3:00pm in the Base Village, Mid Village and Upper Village lots. Payment does not guarantee a space, and all lots are first come, first served.

  • Stilson Lot: The Village Road Transit Center is free to park in, about 7 miles from the Village, with free shuttles to Teton Village.
  • START Bus: From the town of Jackson, a bus takes you right to the base.
  • Overnight: Overnight parking in the Village lots is for hotel guests only.

The Teton Village Association says its JH Insider app shows real time parking availability. Open it before you commit to the Village on a busy morning.

Before opening day on November 27

As of today, November 15, Jackson Hole has not opened. The resort has announced Friday, November 27 as opening day for 2026-27, and its pass page lists April 4, 2027 as closing day. Local lodging sites note that opening dates and available terrain always depend on snow and mountain conditions. Check jacksonhole.com before you drive, and do not assume every lift is spinning on opening weekend.

One more thing that affects your wait: the resort is managing daily mountain capacity again this season. Ikon Pass and Mountain Collective holders need reservations, while Jackson Hole season passholders do not. Confirm your own pass rules on the resort's site, because they can change.

FAQ

How long is the tram wait at Jackson Hole?

It depends on the day. The Aerial Tram is rated at 650 skiers per hour, and local guides say first thing in the morning, after lunch and on powder days are the busiest. Some locals show up around 6:00am for first box.

Is the Bridger Gondola or Sweetwater better?

They do different jobs. The Bridger Gondola is rated at 2,400 skiers per hour. Sweetwater has Lower and Upper stages, each rated at 2,000, with Solitude Station at the mid-station. Both lead to moderate groomed terrain.

When does Jackson Hole open for 2026-27?

The resort has announced Friday, November 27, 2026, with closing day listed as April 4, 2027. Terrain depends on snow and conditions, so check jacksonhole.com before you travel.

Do I need a reservation to ski Jackson Hole?

For 2026-27, Ikon Pass and Mountain Collective holders need reservations because the resort is managing daily capacity. Jackson Hole season passholders do not. Check the resort's site for current rules.

Is parking free at Teton Village?

Not during the day in winter, unless you carpool with four or more in the Ranch Lot, are a hotel guest, or use short-term or ADA parking. Parking is free after 1:00pm in the Ranch Lot and after 3:00pm in the other lots. The Stilson Lot is free, with free shuttles.
