Colorado Ski Resort Opening Dates for 2026-27, Resort by Resort

Colorado's 2026-27 season is already running. Arapahoe Basin opened Oct. 28, Keystone followed on Oct. 30, and Wolf Creek opened on Halloween for a short stretch. Here is every resort's opening day in one place, which ones still have not announced, and what a few groomed runs on man-made snow actually feel like.

Open today, Nov. 1: Arapahoe Basin, Keystone and Wolf Creek

The season started earlier than most of us expected. In the PeakWait app today, three Colorado resorts show as open, and each one is running a thin slice of the mountain.

  • Arapahoe Basin: opened Wednesday, Oct. 28, at 11 a.m. with Black Mountain Express serving High Noon, an intermediate run. It is the resort's 80th season. The app shows 1 lift running. Parking reservations are not required in the early season. A-Basin plans to require them only on Saturdays and Sundays from Jan. 2 through May 2, 2027.
  • Keystone: opened Friday, Oct. 30, with more than 2 miles on Schoolmarm and Silver Spoon, plus a hike-to terrain park. You ride the River Run Gondola up, lap Montezuma Express, and download the gondola at the end of the day. The app shows 2 lifts running, which fits that setup.
  • Wolf Creek: opened Saturday, Oct. 31. The resort says it is open through Tuesday, Nov. 3, closed Wednesday and Thursday, then open Friday through Sunday until significant snow arrives. Lifts run 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.

Loveland is the odd one. The resort has not named an opening day. It says it is aiming for late October to early November, weather permitting. The status the app shows for Loveland is our estimate, not a word from the resort, so check skiloveland.com before you load the car.

Every announced opening day, in order

These are the dates the resorts themselves have published. Every one is a target, and the snow gets a vote.

  • Nov. 6: Breckenridge and Copper Mountain
  • Nov. 13: Eldora and Vail
  • Nov. 20: Powderhorn and Purgatory
  • Nov. 25: Beaver Creek, Crested Butte and Granby Ranch
  • Nov. 26: Aspen Mountain and Snowmass
  • Dec. 9: Ski Cooper
  • Dec. 11: Sunlight Mountain
  • Dec. 12: Aspen Highlands and Buttermilk

One catch. A few roundups list a day off for Aspen Mountain, Eldora, Granby Ranch, Aspen Highlands and Buttermilk. The app follows what each resort published, and resorts do move dates, so check the resort's own site the week you go.

Thanksgiving is Thursday, Nov. 26. The openers on Nov. 25 and 26 are landing right where the first real crowd of the year shows up.

Resorts with no announced date yet

For these, the app only has our own estimate. That is not a date, and it is not proof the resort is open. Here is what I could find from the resorts and from news roundups.

  • Loveland: no date. The resort is targeting late October to early November. Last season it opened Nov. 8 with Chet's Dream spinning.
  • Winter Park: lists its first day as as soon as possible. Our estimate is around Nov. 15.
  • Steamboat: our estimate is around Nov. 22. News roundups list a Nov. 21 opening day, with a T-shirt for the first 100 people on the mountain. Confirm on the resort's site.
  • Telluride: our estimate is around Nov. 27. News roundups say the resort is targeting Nov. 26 and that the date can change with weather.
  • Monarch Mountain: our estimate is around Nov. 20. One Denver roundup lists early to mid December. I did not find a firm date from the resort.
  • Echo Mountain: our estimate is around Nov. 20. Powder lists it as to be determined.
  • Silverton Mountain: our estimate is around Dec. 26. Powder lists Dec. 31 for the guided season.

Where our estimate and a news date disagree, trust neither until the resort posts it.

What early season terrain is really like

Think one or two lifts, a handful of groomed runs, and a mountain that is mostly closed above you.

  • A-Basin and Keystone: A-Basin opened with one run. Keystone opened with about 43 acres. Colorado resorts started making snow on Oct. 15, then warm weather stopped A-Basin and Loveland for several days. A-Basin's own staff say snowmaking is what gets them open this early.
  • Breckenridge and Copper, Nov. 6: usually a few groomed runs on man-made snow. More opens through November and December, and most of the mountain is typically open by Christmas.
  • Vail, Nov. 13: in recent years the opening schedule has started with Ramshorn and Swingsville off Chair 4. You ride Gondola One to Mid-Vail and download it to leave. Vail has more than 5,300 acres including the Back Bowls, but those need far more snow than the groomers. Do not plan a bowl day for opening week.
  • Wolf Creek: this one is natural snow. A storm on Oct. 23 and 24 dropped 20 inches, and the resort reports about 12 inches settled midway. The resort also warns of rocks, unmarked obstacles and possible open water on any trail.

How crowded early season gets

With one chair spinning, the wait is the whole mountain. Two stories from this week. When A-Basin announced a same-day opening, its base webcam showed a fairly short wait at Black Mountain Express just after 11, which makes sense given the short notice. Two days earlier, a rumored Keystone opening pulled in hordes of hopeful skiers before the resort had confirmed anything. It opened that Friday instead.

Dates announced weeks ahead behave differently. Nov. 6 and Nov. 13 are Fridays that every local has circled. Expect a first chair crowd, a slow shuffle up to a single gondola or chair, and the same few runs getting skied flat by noon.

If you hate a wait, skip the opening day itself. The second weekday of a new resort is usually calmer than the first, and the early season is short on lift lines only when nobody knows you are skiing.

Here is the plan I would follow.

  • Check the resort first: A-Basin announced a same-day opening a little before 8 a.m. for an 11 a.m. start. Look at the resort's own site the morning you go, not at last week's article.

FAQ

Which Colorado ski resort opened first for 2026-27?

Arapahoe Basin opened first on Wednesday, Oct. 28, with Black Mountain Express and the High Noon run. Keystone followed on Friday, Oct. 30, and Wolf Creek opened Saturday, Oct. 31.

When do Breckenridge, Copper and Vail open?

Breckenridge and Copper Mountain are both scheduled for Friday, Nov. 6. Vail is scheduled for Friday, Nov. 13. All three dates depend on weather and snowmaking, so check each resort's site close to the day.

Is Loveland open yet?

Loveland has not announced an opening day. The resort says it is targeting late October to early November, weather permitting. The status in the app is our estimate only, so confirm on the resort's website before you drive.

How much terrain is open on opening day?

Very little. Arapahoe Basin opened with one lift and one run, Keystone with about 43 acres, and Wolf Creek with roughly 30 percent of the mountain. Bigger resorts like Vail usually start with a few groomed runs on man-made snow.

Will the lifts be crowded early in the season?

They can be. When only one or two lifts run, everyone shares them, and announced Friday openers draw a first chair crowd. A surprise same-day opening usually means a shorter wait than a date announced weeks ahead.

Do opening dates change?

Yes. Every date here is a target. A resort can open early when snow arrives, as A-Basin did on short notice, or slip a day or more if it stays warm. Treat the resort's own site as the final word.
