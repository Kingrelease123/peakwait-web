First Chair at Arapahoe Basin: When to Arrive and Where to Start

A-Basin sits 68 miles west of downtown Denver, and your day goes to whoever beats the parking lot and the first loading wait. When the lifts start turning

The resort's own operating hours page lists winter lifts at 9 a.m. Monday through Friday. On Saturday, Sunday and holidays the main lifts start at 8:30 a.m. Most of the mountain runs until 4 p.m. Lenawee Express and Pallavicini stop at 3:30, and Beavers and Zuma stop at 3:15.

The same page shows Black Mountain Express, Lenawee Express and Pallavicini starting at 8:30 on weekends. Beavers and Zuma are listed at 9 a.m. daily. So on a Saturday, the first chair is not the same lift for everyone.

Early season does not always follow that page. Opening day this year was announced the day before, with Black Mountain Express planned for 11 a.m. It actually started turning at 10:30. When Lenawee Express opened on November 20, the resort said mid-morning, after patrol finished its final checks. If a lift is new this week, treat the posted time as a floor and check the resort's terrain status the morning you drive up.

Where things stand this week

A-Basin opened for the season on October 28 with one lift, Black Mountain Express, and one run, High Noon. That is the central four-person chair. On November 20 the resort added Lenawee Express, which gave skiers a top-to-bottom route on Lenawee Face, Dercum's Gulch and High Noon. All three are blue runs. The resort also opened uphill access outside operating hours that evening.

  • Confirmed open: the PeakWait app shows A-Basin open for the season, with lifts closed for the night as of today.
  • Not assumed: Pallavicini Lift, Zuma Lift and Beavers. Pallavicini opened on December 5 two seasons ago and in mid-January last season, so do not count on it until the resort says so.
  • Beginners: the Molly Hogan Lift and the carpets are the learning terrain. News coverage this month advised beginners to wait for easier terrain to open.

This is Thanksgiving week. Forecasts called for snow over the holiday weekend, so expect more people than a normal late November Monday.

How early the lots fill

A-Basin is on Highway 6 over Loveland Pass. If the pass is closed, the resort says you can still get there via I-70 and the Eisenhower Tunnel. That is the part of the drive you cannot control, so leave Denver with slack.

The four main lots are Early Riser, High Noon, Last Chance and Upper Last Chance. Before reservations existed, they reached capacity on most weekend and holiday mornings. The resort fills Early Riser first and works up the hill, with Upper Last Chance last. Early Riser is the one you want, because it is closest to the lifts.

  • Weekdays: parking in the main lots is free and needs no reservation.
  • Weekends this winter: reservations are required on Saturdays and Sundays from January 2 through May 2, 2027. On those days the resort says lots typically fill by midmorning.
  • Right now: no reservation is needed before January 2. That means first come, first served, so treat a holiday weekend like the old days.
  • Highway 6: do not park on it. The resort says State Patrol will tow you.

If you would rather skip the lot, last season's reservation announcement pointed skiers to Bustang from Denver Union Station, Federal Center RTD Station and the Wooly Mammoth Park and Ride. Check the current schedule before you count on it.

My rule is to be parked and booted before the first lift turns. That means about an hour before the posted time on a weekend. It is advice, not a number from the resort.

The first two or three lifts to ride

Here is the order that keeps you out of the worst lift lines.

  1. Black Mountain Express. It is the lift that has run since day one, and it is the only one that gets you up from the base on opening day. Be at the loading area before the bell. The top gets you to mid-mountain and the start of everything else.
  2. Lenawee Express. It is a six-person detachable chair, so it moves a lot of people at once. The resort's trail map says most intermediate routes run through Black Mountain Express and Lenawee. Ride it on your second lap, not your tenth, because the crowd stacks up here as the morning goes on.
  3. Pallavicini Lift, once it opens. It is the steep, mostly double-black terrain, reached from the base with one lift ride. Everyone who came for first chair heads here. On a weekend it starts at 8:30, so ride it early, before Zuma Lift and Beavers have even started.

Zuma Lift and Beavers matter later in the season, when they open. They start at 9 a.m., so they are a second-hour move, not a first-chair one.

If it is only one lift

With only Black Mountain Express running, every skier on the mountain is on one chair and one run. There is no second lift to dodge to. The loading wait at the base is simply the whole crowd, and it does not drain, because a short lap puts everyone back at the same spot. On opening day this year, skiers waited to load the chair, and a few drove up from Boulder after seeing the announcement that morning.

Here is what that means for you.

  • Be early. The first laps are the shortest waits you will get.
  • Pick the quiet day. Weekdays have free parking and a 9 a.m. start, and no one is racing a reservation.
  • Expect it to stay small. A-Basin usually makes a handful of major openings in its first two months, then ramps up near the New Year.

With two lifts, the load splits and the wait gets easier. It is still a short mountain, so do not expect miracles.

What to actually do

  • Check the night before. Look at the resort's terrain status and hours for the lifts you want, then set your alarm for parking, not the bell.
  • Park in the Early Riser lot if you can. Weekdays are free. Carpool if you can, and if you are driving up for a Saturday after January 2, book the reservation first.
  • Use PeakWait on the hill. It shows which A-Basin lifts are open and how fresh each wait report is. After your first lap on Black Mountain Express, check which chair it points to next, and report the wait yourself as Moving, Short, Busy or Long. It is free to check lifts and report waits.

FAQ

What time does first chair start at Arapahoe Basin?

The resort's operating hours page lists winter lifts at 9 a.m. Monday through Friday and 8:30 a.m. on Saturday, Sunday and holidays. Beavers and Zuma are listed at 9 a.m. daily. In early season, newly opened lifts can start later than that, so check the resort's terrain status the morning you go.

Do I need a parking reservation at A-Basin?

Not right now. The resort says reservations are only required on Saturdays and Sundays from January 2 through May 2, 2027. Weekday parking in the main lots is free and needs no reservation. Check the resort's parking page for holiday exceptions.

Which lifts are open at Arapahoe Basin?

Black Mountain Express has run since opening day on October 28. Lenawee Express opened on November 20. Pallavicini Lift, Zuma Lift and Beavers were not confirmed open in the sources I read, so check the resort's terrain status before you go.

When does Pallavicini usually open?

It varies a lot. The lift opened on December 5 two seasons ago and in mid-January last season. Do not plan a Pali morning until the resort announces it.

How early do the A-Basin lots fill?

Before reservations, the four main lots reached capacity on most weekend and holiday mornings. On reservation days the resort says lots typically fill by midmorning. Early Riser fills first and Upper Last Chance fills last.

Is A-Basin good for beginners in early season?

Not at the start. Opening day had no beginner terrain, and the top-to-bottom route opened on November 20 is all blue runs. The Molly Hogan Lift and the carpets are the learning terrain. Check the resort's status to see whether they are running.
