Snowbird or Alta on a Busy Day: Tram Waits, Collins and Wildcat, Interlodge

Alta and Snowbird share one road, one canyon and a habit of filling up on the same mornings. On a busy day the real question is which wait you would rather stand in. Here is how the tram wait, the Collins and Wildcat waits, road closures and interlodge change the answer.

The short answer

Go to Snowbird when you want choices. Go to Alta when you want to be skiing by 9:30. Snowbird covers 2,500 acres, has a tram, and has a tunnel into a whole other bowl. Alta is smaller, skiers only, and sends most of its morning through two lifts.

Alta's own site says snowboarding is not allowed. If your group has a snowboarder in it, the decision is already made. If everyone skis, keep reading.

One note on timing. Ski Utah lists Alta's anticipated opening as November 20 and Snowbird's as November 27. Both depend on conditions, so check each resort's own site before you plan around a date.

You can also ski between the two on a joint pass, weather permitting. That matters on a busy day, because it gives you a second set of lift lines to choose from. Here is the Alta Lodge note on the joint pass.

The Snowbird tram wait

The tram is the lift everyone wants to ride and the one everyone ends up waiting for. On powder days people start showing up around 8:30 am. SnowPak says the wait can run from 30 minutes to an hour on busy days. The ride itself covers about 2,900 vertical feet in roughly seven minutes, so the math feels a little rude.

The usual dodge is Peruvian Express, which Snowbird itself nicknames P-Dog. Plenty of locals skip the tram on powder days and lap Peruvian instead. It also gets you to the tunnel, an underground conveyor that drops you toward Mineral Basin while everyone else is still standing at the tram.

Storm mornings add a twist. Snowbird warns that Gadzoom, Peruvian and the tram can all be delayed while avalanche control happens. Its advice is to lap Wilbere until the upper mountain opens, rather than wait at a closed rope. Mineral Basin Express can also back up once people reach the back bowl.

  • Tram: the wait builds from about 8:30 on powder days. Ride it when the upper mountain is actually open.
  • Peruvian Express: the standard way around a long tram wait, and your route to the tunnel.
  • Wilbere: the storm morning fallback while mitigation work finishes.
  • Baldy Express: mostly used as the link over to Alta, so it is a good way to change your day.

The Collins and Wildcat waits at Alta

Alta has fewer lifts to spread people out, so a few of them take the whole morning. Collins and Wildcat both load from the Wildcat base. On powder days SnowPak says people are waiting for both by 8 am, well before lifts start turning at 9:15. OnTheSnow tells the same story about locals showing up at Collins by 8.

Alta limits its parking through reservations. The resort says that is partly to manage lift capacity, which is a polite way of saying it tries not to let in more skiers than the mountain can move. That helps, but it does not make Collins empty on a deep morning.

The best trick is timing. SnowPak says weekend and holiday waits tend to show up between 11 am and 2:30 pm, but they rarely form at Wildcat. OnTheSnow suggests skiing Wildcat between 1 and 3 pm, when most people have drifted over to Supreme and Sugarloaf.

Do not assume a powder morning opens on time. KUTV reported one early season storm where Alta pushed its opening to 11 am for avalanche control. Sugarloaf, Collins and Wildcat opened first, and skiers waited hours for the lift. Expect that kind of morning on a big storm day and you will not be surprised.

Road closures and getting up the canyon

Both resorts sit on SR-210, and neither controls it. UDOT counts 64 slide paths in Little Cottonwood Canyon. It fully closes the road to the public while avalanche mitigation is in progress. Snowbird says it will warn guests about a midday closure through its app, digital signs and the Mountain Report.

Traction law is the other gate. Snowbird says that if it is snowing on SR-210, the law will almost certainly be in effect. Two-wheel drive vehicles need 3PMSF tires on all four wheels, or chains. Alta adds that state law requires snow tires or carried chains from November 1 to May 1. Police at the canyon entrance can turn a car around.

Parking is where the two resorts differ most. Alta runs a parking reservation system for winter 2026 to 27, with new reservations released every Sunday at 3 pm Mountain time. Snowbird says roadside parking on the south side of SR-210 between Entries 1 and 4 is first come, first served, and only when allowed that day. Overnight parking along the road is prohibited. UDOT also bans parking in marked avalanche zones from November 1 to May 15.

The UTA ski bus is free with a season pass to Snowbird, Alta, Brighton or Solitude, and with the Ikon Pass. Check the UDOT Cottonwood Canyons page and the Snowbird getting here page before you leave.

Interlodge: when nobody leaves the building

Interlodge is the part nobody warns you about. When avalanche danger gets serious, it becomes illegal to leave the shelter of your building. Ski Utah says interlodge usually lasts a couple of hours. The record at Alta is 60 hours in February 2021, with 103 inches of snow.

Snowbird can go on interlodge too. When an avalanche hit SR-210 near Entry 4 and caught three vehicles, KUTV reported that Snowbird Village was placed on interlodge while crews worked, then lifted it after the road was cleared. Plan for an afternoon where you suddenly have a long lunch.

What comes after matters for your wait. When Alta lifted its 60 hour interlodge, Collins and Wildcat were the first lifts to start carrying skiers. In January 2023 the Salt Lake Tribune reported Collins, Wildcat and Sugarloaf as the first to open after an interlodge. So the morning after, those same two lifts are the busiest ones at Alta. On the Snowbird side, expect a rush on whatever opens first.

Read the Alta story on the 60 hour interlodge if you want to see what a full lockdown looks like.

What to actually do

Here is how to turn all of that into a plan for a busy day.

  • Check the road first: look at UDOT road status and the resort's mountain report before you leave the valley. At Alta, grab a parking reservation. For Snowbird, plan on the bus or an early arrival.
  • Pick your first lift by the wait: at Snowbird, start on Peruvian or Wilbere instead of the tram. At Alta, start on Wildcat and move to Supreme or Sugarloaf as the morning goes on.
  • Use PeakWait to choose the next chair: skiers report live waits as Moving, Short, Busy or Long, with how fresh each report is, and the app points you to the best next chair. FAQ

Is Snowbird or Alta less crowded on a powder day?

Neither is empty. Alta's morning is concentrated on Collins and Wildcat, with people waiting by 8 am. Snowbird has a tram wait that can be avoided by lapping Peruvian Express. Alta limits skiers through its parking reservations, while Snowbird has more lifts to spread people out.

Can I ski both Alta and Snowbird in one day?

Yes, with a joint Alta-Snowbird pass, weather permitting. Baldy Express at Snowbird links the two. Snowboarders can ride Snowbird but cannot ride Alta, since Alta does not allow snowboarding.

What time does the Snowbird tram wait start building?

On powder days people start showing up around 8:30 am, according to SnowPak. The same source says the wait can run 30 minutes to an hour on busy days. Peruvian Express is the usual way around it.

Do I need chains or snow tires to drive up Little Cottonwood Canyon?

Often, yes. The UDOT Traction Law applies when there is snow on SR-210. Two-wheel drive vehicles need 3PMSF tires on all four wheels, or chains. State law requires snow tires or carried chains from November 1 to May 1, and police can turn vehicles around at the canyon entrance.

What is interlodge?

It is a safety order that makes it illegal to leave the shelter of your building during serious avalanche danger. Ski Utah says it usually lasts a couple of hours, though Alta's record is 60 hours in February 2021. Both Alta and Snowbird Village can be placed on interlodge.

When do Alta and Snowbird open this season?

Ski Utah lists Alta's anticipated opening as November 20 and Snowbird's as November 27. Both are conditions permitting, and Snowbird calls its date tentative. Check each resort's own site for the latest.
