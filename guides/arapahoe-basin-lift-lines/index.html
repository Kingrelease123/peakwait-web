Arapahoe Basin Lift Lines: First Chair, Beavers and Parking

By Herb Sendit · 5 min read · November 5, 2026

Arapahoe Basin opened for 2026-27 on October 28 with one chair and one run, which is exactly when the Black Mountain Express wait gets interesting. Here is how first chair season works, what changes when the Beavers and Steep Gullies come into play, how to handle weekend parking in the Early Riser lot, and why the spring season is worth planning around. It comes from someone who has stood in these waits with cold toes.

First chair season at A-Basin

The Basin opened for the season on Wednesday, October 28, at 11 a.m. Black Mountain Express was the only lift spinning and High Noon was the only run. Hundreds of people showed up anyway, because that is what an October opening does to a Colorado skier.

PeakWait shows A-Basin as open for the season today. Early on, the resort adds terrain as conditions allow, so what is running this week may not match what ran last week. Check the resort's own site for the current lift list before you drive up Loveland Pass.

This is also A-Basin's 80th season, and Ikon Pass and Ikon Base Pass holders have unlimited access. Expect a crowd of people who have been waiting since May.

Black Mountain Express: the one chair everybody shares

Black Mountain Express is a four-person detachable chair. It showed up for the 2010-11 season and replaced the old Exhibition lift, and it was the mountain's first detachable. It takes you to mid-mountain and Black Mountain Lodge, and it is where the greens and most of the blues start.

In the first weeks of the season the math is simple. One chair serves the whole mountain, so lift lines build on weekends and around late morning. The Early Riser lot sits right next to the lift and the Winter Sports Building, which means everyone who parks there also reaches the loading area at the same moment.

My rule is to be on the chair early or to ride it while everyone else is eating. A midweek day in October feels like a different mountain than a Saturday.

Pali, Lenawee, Zuma and the learning area

When more of the mountain opens, the waits spread out. The Pallavicini Lift serves mostly black and double black terrain on the west side, including the steep mogul runs of the Pali face. Zuma Lift takes you to the south-facing backside bowl, which has about 400 acres of intermediate groomers, trees and cliffs.

Lenawee Express matters more than people think. On the last weekend of last season, Black Mountain Express and Lenawee were both running, and that made the mountain skiable top to bottom.

Beginners usually start at Molly Hogan and the two carpets, Molly's Magic Carpet and Pika Place. After that, the easy runs are mostly off Black Mountain Express.

The Beavers and Steep Gullies

The Beavers added more than 300 acres in 2017-18, and the Beavers chair opened that terrain to lifts in 2018-19. It is a fixed-grip quad. Don't expect it to chew through a crowd the way a high-speed chair does. The resort says it reaches 36 runs, from rolling groomers to glades and chutes.

The Steep Gullies are a different animal. They are expert-only, steep, narrow and rocky. There are two ways in. Ride the Pallavicini Lift, then traverse the Pali Cornice toward the Alleys to the gate for Gullies 1, 2 and 3. Or take the Eastwood gates toward the Beavers, turn right and follow the ridge to the gate for Gullies 4 through 8.

You do not ride out. The Gullies end with a hike of roughly 20 to 30 minutes back to the bottom of the Pallavicini Lift. That means a Gullies day is really a Pali wait day, and a powder morning is when it is busiest. The Gullies open when conditions allow, so check the resort before you commit.

Parking in the Early Riser lot

The Early Riser lot is the closest general lot to the base and the lifts. It has EV charging and accessible spaces. The Beach is the slopeside strip of this lot where people tailgate, and it needs its own reservation this season.

For 2026-27, parking reservations are not needed on opening day or in the early season. They start on Saturdays and Sundays from January 2 through May 2, 2027. On those days the reservation covers 6 a.m. to 1 p.m., and parking after 1 p.m. is free with no reservation. Weekday parking in Early Riser stays free and first come, first served.

Carpools with four or more people park free in Early Riser, High Noon, Last Chance and Upper Last Chance, but they still need a reservation. The lot operator says the lots typically fill by midmorning on reservation days, and cars get turned back. Book through parkabasin.com and don't park on Highway 6, because the State Patrol tows.

If Loveland Pass is closed, the resort says you can still get here via I-70 and the Eisenhower Tunnel. Public transit is another option.

The long spring season

A-Basin brags about the longest season in Colorado, and last year it earned it even in a bad snow year. It opened on October 26, 2025, planned to close on May 3, then extended to May 10 and again to May 17 after storms. That was 194 days on snow.

In a normal year the season runs into June. Back in 2018-19 it ran until July 4, and the latest closing ever was August 10, 1995.

Spring is when the Beach earns its reputation, with afternoons spent in the Early Riser lot. As the snow shrinks, fewer lifts spin, so the waits pile up at whatever is still open. On that final weekend in May, lifts ran 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Friday and 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Saturday and Sunday. Closing dates get announced late and extended, so trust the resort's site over any calendar.

FAQ

When did Arapahoe Basin open for 2026-27?
A-Basin opened on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, at 11 a.m. with Black Mountain Express and the High Noon run. The app shows it open for the season, but check the resort's site for which lifts are running now.
Do I need a parking reservation at A-Basin?
Only on Saturdays and Sundays from January 2 through May 2, 2027, between 6 a.m. and 1 p.m. Weekdays are free and first come, first served. After 1 p.m. on weekends parking is free with no reservation.
How do I get into the Steep Gullies?
Ride the Pallavicini Lift. Then take the Pali Cornice to the gate for Gullies 1 through 3, or the Eastwood gates and the ridge for Gullies 4 through 8. They are expert-only and end with a hike of about 20 to 30 minutes back to the Pallavicini Lift.
How long is the A-Basin ski season?
It is the longest in Colorado. Last season it ran 194 days, from October 26, 2025 to May 17, 2026. In bigger snow years it has gone into June and July.
How can I check A-Basin lift waits?
Skiers report live waits as Moving, Short, Busy or Long, and the app shows them on every lift with how fresh each report is. Checking and reporting are free.
