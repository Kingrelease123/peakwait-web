Killington Lift Waits: Where the Waits Build and How to Beat Them

Killington spreads six peaks across 22 lifts, and the waits pile up in a few predictable spots. Here is where they build, which chair to take instead, and how the long season from November to May changes the answer. The season is also just starting, so the first thing to sort out is what is actually open.

First, what is actually open this week

Dates move, so start here. The PeakWait app today shows Killington opening around Nov 20, but that is only our estimate and nothing from the resort backs it. Killington says something different. It opened Nov. 17 for Killington, Beast 365 and Ikon passholders, and the general public got in Wednesday, Nov. 18 at 9 a.m. Trust the resort over our estimate, and check the mountain report at killington.com before you drive.

Early season here is a two-lift show. The K-1 Express Gondola and North Ridge Quad serve Rime, Reason and Upper East Fall on North Ridge, and there is no beginner terrain yet. On opening day the last ride was 3 p.m. on K-1 and 3:30 p.m. on North Ridge. You also have to take the Peak Walkway to and from the gondola. Two lifts for a whole weekend crowd means real lift lines, so be patient and be early.

Six peaks means six different waits

The resort is spread over six peaks: Killington Peak, Skye Peak, Snowdon, Ramshead, Bear Mountain and Sunrise. That is why one lift can have a long wait while the chair two ridges over is empty. PeakWait tracks 22 lifts here, from gondolas and six-packs down to magic carpets and a Poma surface lift for training.

The simplest habit is to pick a peak and work it. One local guide says to orient by peaks, not trails, and not crisscross the whole resort. Every connector you ski to chase a shorter wait costs you the time you hoped to save.

If you do want to skip the queue, Killington sells Fast Tracks, an all-day upgrade with its own lane. The lifts with a Fast Tracks lane include the K-1 Express Gondola, Superstar, Ramshead Express, Snowshed Express, Bear Mountain, Skye Peak Express, both Skyeship stages, Snowdon Six and Needles Eye Express. Ask the resort what it costs.

The K-1 Gondola is the morning magnet

The K-1 Express Gondola carries you 1.25 miles to the 4,241 foot summit of Killington Peak, the highest lift-served point in Vermont. It moves about 2,800 skiers per hour, which sounds like plenty until a Saturday at 9 a.m. When everyone parks at K-1 Lodge and wants the summit, you will stand in a wait.

It also has a nice trick. The K-1 base is the highest base, so it is the first to open and last to close in a long season. From the summit you can ski to any other lift on the mountain, which makes it a decent way to dodge a crowded base.

Two small notes. When there is no skiable route back to the K-1 base, you use the Peak Walkway stairs to get back to the gondola. And the Peak Lodge at the top gets crowded on busy days, so eat a little before or after the usual noon rush.

Skyeship and the Route 4 side

The Skyeship Express Gondola runs in two stages, from the base on Route 4 up to Skye Peak, with a stop at Needle's Eye. Skyeship base is the first one you see driving in from the east, so it is popular with Boston visitors. It is also typically the least crowded base, and a good pick when you arrive late and still want to park close to a lift.

The catch is that the gondola is the only lift out of that base. If the wait builds, you cannot just hop on a different chair. Needle's Eye is not a base at all. It has no parking or lodge, just a valley where trails and lifts meet, so you pass through it more than you start there.

Three lifts end at the top of Skye Peak, including the Skyeship Gondola and the Skye Peak Express. That gives you options up top when one has a longer wait than the others.

Ramshead, Snowshed and where to park

Ramshead is the mellow, family-friendly side. It is also one of the classic places for a long wait, because that area is crowded on weekends with only one lift. If you are skiing with kids, expect a wait there on a Saturday, and bring snacks.

Snowshed has more to work with, since there is both a Snowshed Express Quad and a Snowshed Double. If one has a wait, check the other.

On parking, Killington says free parking is available at all base lodges, with premium parking at K-1 and Snowshed in winter. One local guide suggests that on weekends you park at Bear or Skyeship bases to skip the main lodge crush. Overnight parking and camping are allowed only in the Skyeship lot on Route 4 from November through May.

November to May changes the math

Killington often stays open into May and, in some years, into June. Pico, its sister mountain on the same tickets, typically closes in early April. Late in the season, terrain is generally limited to intermediate and advanced trails.

That shapes your waits. Early and late, only a few lifts spin, so the ones that run carry everyone, and K-1 plus North Ridge are the usual suspects. In midwinter, with the whole system open, the crowd spreads out and waits become more local, so the right chair changes by the hour.

One more change is worth knowing. Superstar was a quad built in 1987, and the resort replaced it with a six-pack. A six-pack moves more people, so if an old trip report says Superstar had a wait, take it with a grain of salt.

What to actually do

  • Check first: Read the resort's mountain report for what is open and which lodge to park at. In PeakWait, look at which lifts are open and how fresh each wait report is before you pick a base.
  • Report as you ride: When you load, tap Moving, Short, Busy or Long. It is free, and the next skier will know whether to take Ramshead or walk to Snowshed.
  • Plan the day: On weekends, park at Skyeship or Bear, ride K-1 early or after lunch, and let the app point you to the best next chair instead of guessing.

FAQ

Is Killington open right now?

The resort says it opened Nov. 17 for passholders and to the general public on Wednesday, Nov. 18 at 9 a.m. Early on, only the K-1 Express Gondola and North Ridge Quad are running, with no beginner terrain. Check the mountain report at killington.com for today's lifts.

Which Killington lift has the longest wait?

It changes by day and hour. Local guides point to the Ramshead area on weekends, since it has one lift, and to the K-1 Gondola on busy mornings. Skyeship base is usually the least crowded, though it has only the gondola.

What is Fast Tracks at Killington?

It is an all-day paid upgrade that gives you an express lane at the resort's most popular lifts, including the K-1 Gondola, Superstar, Ramshead Express and both Skyeship stages. Check killington.com for current details.

When does Killington close for the season?

There is no fixed date. It often stays open into May, and some years into June. Pico typically closes in early April. Watch the resort's site for the closing date.

Does the Ikon Pass work at Killington?

Yes. Killington says Ikon is direct-to-lift at Killington and Pico, and skiing both on the same day uses one Ikon day. Check your pass terms for days and blackout dates.
