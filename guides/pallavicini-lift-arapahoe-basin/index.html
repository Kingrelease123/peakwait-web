The Pallavicini Lift at Arapahoe Basin: The Wait and When to Ride It

The Pallavicini lift at Arapahoe Basin is a slow, old-school double chair with a huge reputation, and the wait for it can swell fast on weekends and powder mornings. Here is what it serves, who it is for, when the wait builds, and which other A-Basin chairs to ride while Pali is busy.

What the Pallavicini lift serves

Pali, as everyone calls it, is a fixed-grip double chair on the front side of Arapahoe Basin. The current lift is a Leitner-Poma that replaced the original 1978 chair, and it opened to riders on December 19, 2020. It moves up to 1,200 people an hour, the same as the old one, and the ride takes about seven minutes.

That is slow on purpose. A-Basin's president and COO has said the resort passed on a fancy high-speed chair and kept a simple double. A slow chair keeps the crowd on the face small, which is part of why people love it.

The terrain off the top is mostly double black. When the lift is fully open you get runs like Slalom Slope, Grizzly Road, Powder Keg, West Wall, North Chute and South Chute. Expect steep moguls and tight trees. The payoff is a short ride for a lot of vertical, so you can lap it all day.

Who Pali is for

  • Strong skiers: If you are comfortable on steep bumps and in the trees, this is your chair.
  • Lap people: One lift, many runs, and a base area where you can walk from the parking lot to the chair.
  • Not for beginners: If you are still learning, the Molly Hogan chair and the carpets are the right place to start.

Is Pali running today?

Do not assume it is. The PeakWait app shows Arapahoe Basin as open for the season and confirmed, with lifts closed for the night. That does not mean every chair is spinning. Pali usually opens well after the first lifts, once Grizzly Road and West Wall have enough snow.

A Summit Daily report from November 24, 2026 said Molly Hogan was the third of A-Basin's nine lifts to open this season, after Black Mountain Express and Lenawee Express. Pali was not on that list. Things move fast in early season, so check the resort's own site at arapahoebasin.com before you drive up.

Past openings show how much it varies. Pali opened on December 25 in 2023. It opened in early December in 2024. Last season it waited until Saturday, January 10, 2026, and it started turning around noon. A swarm showed up for that one, which tells you how people feel about this chair.

When the wait builds on a weekend

Weekends are when the base area gets crowded. The resort requires a parking reservation for every vehicle on Saturdays and Sundays from January 2 to May 2, 2027, for arrivals between 6 a.m. and 1 p.m. The resort says the lots typically fill by midmorning on busy days. Weekday parking is free and first come, first served.

The Beach is the front-row lot right beside the bottom of the frontside lifts, and it needs a reservation every day. The resort even pitches it as the spot for stacking up Pali laps. If you park there, you are about ten steps from the wait, and so is everyone else who did.

The resort's hours page lists weekend lifts starting at 8:30 a.m. and weekday lifts at 9 a.m. for Lenawee Express. Check the posted time for Pali itself. One writer who rode here said the crowd gathers about half an hour before a weekend opening.

A little arithmetic helps. A lift that moves up to 1,200 people an hour clears about 20 people a minute. So a few hundred people stacked up at the base is a wait of ten minutes or more, and it only shrinks once the first rush has been loaded. The rush is usually the start of the day. Skiers who arrive later often find a shorter wait, but the lot may be full by then, so plan around the reservation.

The powder day wait

This is the big one. One ski writer put it this way: on a powder day, around 100 people can be waiting at the base for the chair to start moving. Locals gather at the Pali base on powder days for the steeps, and the lot is close, so it is easy to understand.

The same writer pointed out that north-facing runs like Turbo, Spine and Rock Garden can give a good return, since a seven minute ride buys you a long run down. That means people lap Pali hard once it opens, and the chair keeps loading steadily all morning.

Here is how I would play it:

  1. Be at the base before the first chair if you want fresh tracks off the top. Bring a thermos and good humor.
  2. If you miss first chair, do not just get in the wait. Ride Lenawee Express instead, because West Wall and Cornice Run can be reached from both Lenawee and Pali.
  3. Come back to Pali later, when the first wave of skiers has thinned out and spread across the mountain.

A-Basin limits daily lift ticket sales, according to one ski site, so even a powder day tends to avoid the never-ending waits you see at bigger resorts. Another review says even on peak days, waits here rarely run past 10 or 15 minutes. Pali on a deep morning is the exception that tests that rule.

What to ride while Pali is busy

Arapahoe Basin has nine lifts, so you have options. These are the ones worth knowing when the Pali wait is long.

  • Lenawee Express: A six-pack that replaced the old triple in the 2022-23 season. It takes you to the top of the mountain and to West Wall and Cornice Run. It is your best bet to dodge the Pali crowd without leaving steep terrain.
  • Black Mountain Express: The central high-speed four-person chair. It was the lift that opened this season on October 28. Use it for a warm-up lap or when you just want to move.
  • Beavers: A four-person fixed-grip chair with 1,501 vertical feet, serving high-alpine powder fields and gullies. Know that a ride out of here can take as long as 10 minutes.
  • Zuma: Takes you to the Montezuma Bowl on the back side. The Lazy J Tow connects it to Lenawee Express. Beavers and Zuma are listed as opening at 9 a.m. and closing at 3:15 p.m.
  • Molly Hogan and the carpets: The beginner zone. Not the way to dodge a Pali wait, but a good place for friends who do not ski double blacks.

Early in the season, some of these lifts may not be open yet. Look at what is actually spinning before you commit to a plan.

What to actually do

  • Check the wait before you leave the car: Open PeakWait to see which A-Basin lifts are open and the latest rider reports of Moving, Short, Busy or Long, with how fresh each one is.
  • Pick the next chair, not the famous one: If Pali shows Busy or Long, PeakWait points you to the best next chair. Lenawee Express is the usual answer for steep terrain.
  • Report what you see: Reporting a wait is free. If Pali is Long at 9:30, tell the next skier before they park.

On weekends, book your parking reservation first, since that decides whether you get to the base at all. Then arrive early, ride Pali while the crowd is still building, and save the other lifts for later.

FAQ

How long is the wait for the Pallavicini lift?

It depends on the day. Skiers who have ridden it say around 100 people can be waiting at the base before opening on a powder day. A ski review says that even on peak days, waits at A-Basin rarely stretch past 10 or 15 minutes. Check a live report before you go.

Is Pallavicini a high-speed chairlift?

No. It is a fixed-grip double chair that carries up to 1,200 people an hour, and the ride takes about seven minutes. A-Basin chose to keep it a simple double when it replaced the 1978 lift.

Is Pali good for intermediate skiers?

Not really. The terrain it serves is mostly double black, with steep moguls and trees. Intermediates will have more fun on Lenawee Express and Black Mountain Express terrain.

Do I need a parking reservation for A-Basin?

On Saturdays and Sundays from January 2 to May 2, 2027, all vehicles need a general parking reservation for arrivals between 6 a.m. and 1 p.m. Weekday parking is free and first come, first served. The Beach lot needs a reservation every day. Confirm details at arapahoebasin.com.

When does Pali open each season?

It varies with snow. It opened on December 25 in 2023, in early December in 2024, and on January 10 in 2026. This season it had not been listed among the open lifts as of November 24, so check the resort's site.
