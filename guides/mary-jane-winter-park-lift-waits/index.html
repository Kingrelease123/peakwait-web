Mary Jane at Winter Park: Lifts, Lift Waits and When to Go

Mary Jane is the steep, bumpy side of Winter Park. It has its own base, its own parking and its own way of making you wait. Six lifts sit on the Jane itself, and a few more feed it. Check whether Mary Jane is open first

PeakWait shows Winter Park as open for the season, confirmed by the resort's lift feed or by skiers on the mountain. Lifts are closed for the night right now. That status is for Winter Park as a whole, and Mary Jane keeps its own calendar.

The resort opened for the season on November 13. The sources I found say Mary Jane has no fixed date and opens when conditions allow. Winter Park Lodging Company notes that this has historically meant January. Last season the Jane opened on January 9, 2026.

The resort is planning an event for Mary Jane's 51st season the second weekend of January 2027. Its 2026-27 preview also mentions a members-only preview day before the public opening. Check the mountain report at winterparkresort.com before you drive up, because a date can change.

The lifts that serve Mary Jane

The resort's mountain report groups six lifts under Mary Jane. Here is how each one behaves.

  • Super Gauge Express: the six-pack from the Mary Jane base, rated at 3200 skiers per hour. It is the main way up and the main way over to the rest of the resort.
  • Challenger: locals call it the C-Chair. It goes to steeper bumps and trees. It is a fixed-grip chair rated at 1200 skiers per hour, so a short-looking wait can run longer than it looks.
  • Iron Horse: another base lift into the long steep runs. It is rated at 1200 skiers per hour.
  • Pony Express and Galloping Goose: slower lower-mountain chairs, rated at 1200 and 1080 skiers per hour. They are useful when the main chair is backed up.
  • Sunnyside Express: a six-pack rated at 2400 skiers per hour. Outbound Folk says it reaches Parsenn Bowl and some intermediate terrain.

Two more lifts matter even though they are not on the Jane. Hi-Lonesome Express is listed on the Winter Park side. Sky-Hi News describes it as running along the edge of Mary Jane. Eagle Wind, rated at 1800 skiers per hour, is reached through Mary Jane, and Panoramic Express, rated at 3200, takes you to the top of Parsenn Bowl.

Where the wait builds on a weekend

It builds at the bottom. PeakRankings calls the Mary Jane base a chokepoint. Super Gauge is the main way out to the rest of the resort, and the review says it sees serious waits at peak times. The alternatives are slow or out of the way. Outbound Folk agrees that the base area is the congested part and the slopes themselves feel emptier.

Before the lift, there is the parking. The resort says the Mary Jane lots typically fill first on weekends. There is an electronic sign near the entrance that tells you when they are full, so read it before you turn in.

Weekend hours help. The resort is moving to 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Saturdays and Sundays, starting in mid-December or when the Gondola begins running, whichever comes first. Early Ups is now Monday through Friday only, so nobody gets a paid head start on a weekend. Plan to be in the Super Gauge wait before most people have found their gloves, or arrive after the first rush and ski the Jane's steeper side lifts while the base clears.

Sunnyside and Panoramic Express are the lifts that take you toward Parsenn Bowl. Check them before you commit to that side, since a long wait there is a long walk back.

Where the wait builds on a powder day

Everyone wants the same thing, so the Super Gauge wait and the parking both fill early. Outbound Folk says the Mary Jane lots fill very quickly on weekends and powder days. On a weekday, Early Ups holders still get the hour before the public, so expect tracks to go fast.

The usual advice is to spread out. The Avant Ski says to skip Parsenn Bowl on a powder day because it looks better than it skis. It points experts to Eagle Wind instead, where the slower lift keeps tourists away. Eagle Wind is rated at 1800 skiers per hour, well under Super Gauge, so the crowd there is limited by the lift.

If Super Gauge is backed up, Ski Magazine suggests Challenger for more trees and bumps. That fits its capacity as well. Fewer chairs per hour means fewer people on the hill, but only if the wait stays short. Check before you commit.

Moving between Mary Jane and the Winter Park side

You have three ways to do it. Pick the one that fits where you parked.

  1. Park at Mary Jane: you are at Super Gauge in a few minutes and skiing across from the top. Get there early, because those lots fill first.
  2. Park at Winter Park, take the shuttle: the resort runs a free shuttle between the Winter Park base and the Mary Jane base every 30 minutes. The resort's parking page says it leaves Mary Jane at :00 and :30 until 5 p.m. Mary Jane shuttle service starts the day Mary Jane opens for the season.
  3. Ski over: ride the Gondola and ski across. Winter Park Lodging Company's local tip is Sober Englishman or Outhouse. Outhouse is the fastest way over from the Winter Park side and also the hardest, so be sure you want it.

Plan the way home before lunch. PeakRankings points out that if you go up the main mountain lifts, there are no green trails back to the Mary Jane base. For the end of the day, the same lodging company lists Whistlestop and the High Lonesome trail as ways from the top of Mary Jane back to the Winter Park base. There is no overnight parking in either lot, and parking reservations are not required.

What to actually do

  • Confirm Mary Jane first: look at the resort's mountain report for the Jane's date and lift status before you drive. Open Winter Park does not mean open Mary Jane.
  • Pick your side by wait: before you leave the car, open PeakWait. It shows which lifts are open and how fresh each skier report is. If Super Gauge shows Long, start on Challenger or ride the shuttle to the Winter Park side.
  • Report what you see: when you reach a wait, mark it Moving, Short, Busy or Long. It is free to check lifts and report, and the next chair recommendation gets better with every report.

FAQ

Is Mary Jane open right now?

Not that I could confirm. Winter Park opened November 13, and the sources I found say Mary Jane has no fixed date and opens when conditions allow, which has historically meant January. Check the mountain report at winterparkresort.com.

Which lifts serve Mary Jane?

The resort's mountain report lists Challenger, Galloping Goose, Iron Horse, Pony Express, Sunnyside Express and Super Gauge Express under Mary Jane. Hi-Lonesome Express runs along its edge, and Eagle Wind and Panoramic Express are reached through it.

Can I park at Winter Park and still ski Mary Jane?

Yes. The resort runs a free shuttle between the two bases every 30 minutes, and you can also take the Gondola and ski over. Mary Jane shuttle service begins the day Mary Jane opens.

Which Mary Jane lift has the longest wait on a weekend?

Super Gauge Express, the main way out of the Mary Jane base. PeakRankings describes serious waits at peak times, usually busy mornings. Check the current wait on the lift before you commit.

Where should I go on a Mary Jane powder day?

Get to the lots early, since they fill fast. The Avant Ski suggests Eagle Wind and says to skip Parsenn Bowl. Ski Magazine suggests Challenger if the Super Gauge wait is long.
