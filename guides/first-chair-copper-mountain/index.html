First Chair at Copper Mountain: Beat the Lift Waits From I-70

When the lifts start turning

Copper's own lift hours page puts the base area lifts at 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. That list includes Alpine, Super Bee, Pitchfork, Gem, Easy Rider, Rugrat, Slingshot, American Eagle, American Flyer, Woodward Express and Kokomo Express. So 9 a.m. is first chair for the lifts most day skiers from Denver care about.

The upper lifts quit earlier, and that matters more than the opening time. Here is the closing schedule from the same page.

  • 2:30 p.m.: Three Bears.
  • 3 p.m.: Storm King, Resolution, Blackjack and Mountain Chief.
  • 3:30 p.m.: Timberline Express, Excelerator, Sierra, Lumberjack and Rendezvous.

One more detail. Copper sells a Fast Tracks upgrade that gives 30 minutes of early access on American Eagle. You do not need it. Just know that a few people are already on the hill when everyone else is still clicking into bindings.

A posted time is a schedule, not a promise. Wind, snow safety work or a slow snowmaking week can push a lift back. That is exactly what happened to this season's opening day, which slipped two days.

Parking: the free lots and the walk

The free lots are Alpine and Far East. Copper's parking page says they are free seven days a week during winter operating hours. From I-70, take exit 195 onto CO-91 south toward Copper Mountain and Leadville. Go past Copper Road and follow the signs for Alpine or Far East. Copper's visitor page lists those lots as open from 5 a.m. to 10 p.m., so you can leave Denver in the dark and not worry about arriving too early.

The interior lots are paid. They are Ten Mile, Chapel, Union Creek, Beeler and Wheeler. Prices change through the season, you pay with a QR code posted in each lot, and attendants check plates. Paying to park a little closer is not much of a head start, so I would skip it for a day trip.

Getting from the car to the lifts works two ways.

  • Walk: From the Alpine lot, Copper says it is an easy 7-minute walk to East Village.
  • Ride: Copper runs a free in-resort shuttle that serves Center Village, East Village and the two free lots. The visitor page lists winter shuttle hours as 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. Check the signs in the lot for which bus goes where.

Build in boot time. Between the lot, the walk or shuttle, boots and a bathroom stop, getting from the car to a lift can easily eat 45 minutes. Plan backward from 9.

Which base area to start from

Copper has three villages, and the terrain gets steeper as you go from west to east. West Village is where beginners and ski school live, with Kokomo Express and Lumberjack serving the gentle greens. Center Village is the intermediate hub, home to American Eagle and American Flyer. East Village is the advanced side, with the Super Bee and the upper mountain.

For most Front Range day skiers, start in Center Village. It is the middle of everything, and it has the two highest-rated lifts on the mountain side by side. American Eagle is rated at 3400 skiers per hour and American Flyer at 3200. Two big lifts from one base means the crowd splits, and you can pick whichever looks shorter.

Here is how I would choose.

  • Center Village: Your default. Groomers, food and a meeting spot if your group skis at different speeds.
  • East Village: Park in Alpine and walk. This is the pick if you want steeper terrain and the Super Bee, which is rated at 2900 skiers per hour.
  • West Village: Beginners, families and anyone with a lesson. Everyone else can come back in the afternoon for easy laps.

The first three lifts to ride

The hourly ratings below are rated skiers per hour for each chair. A higher rating means a wait clears faster. It does not mean no wait.

  1. American Flyer: Rated 3200 skiers per hour, from Center Village. MountainMap's Copper guide suggests strong intermediates start in Center at opening and lap the Flyer groomers before the crowds arrive. That matches how these mornings tend to go.
  2. American Eagle: Rated 3400 skiers per hour, from the same base. It has gondola cabins mixed in with the chairs. If the Flyer wait looks longer at 9:30, switch here. If the Eagle looks longer, go back.
  3. Super Bee: Rated 2900 skiers per hour, from East Village. The same guide suggests moving east to the Super Bee mid-morning. This is also your way up to the bigger terrain, so get it done while the sun is still low.

If you are a bowl person, save the high lifts for before lunch. Mountain Chief and Blackjack are each rated at 1200 skiers per hour, which is on the slow end, and both stop at 3 p.m. Three Bears is also rated at 1200 and stops at 2:30. If your plan is bowl laps, do them while the clock is on your side.

If you want a mellow blue lap west of the Flyer, Timberline Express is rated at 3200 skiers per hour and serves longer blues. It makes a good fourth ride.

What is actually running this week

This part matters right now. The PeakWait app shows Copper as open for the season, confirmed by lifts running. Copper opened on Sunday, November 8, after a two-day delay for snowmaking. On opening day the mountain revolved around American Eagle, with Rhapsody, Fairplay and Main Vein open. No beginner terrain was open at first.

Summit Daily reported on November 19 that the Easy Rider learning area would open that Saturday. The resort said it hoped to open more terrain before Thanksgiving. A snow report from this week still described very limited terrain, with one lift spinning.

Those reports do not all agree on the exact count, and things change by the day. Before you drive up, check the terrain status on coppercolorado.com. Do not plan a Super Bee morning until the resort lists it open.

Thanksgiving weekend has another wrinkle. Copper hosts the Stifel Copper Cup men's super-G and giant slalom in East Village on November 28 and 29, per Summit Daily. Expect East Village to be busy and expect some restrictions. Check Copper's site for event parking and shuttle details.

What to actually do

Here is the short version for your day.

  • Drive and park: Aim to be at exit 195 by about 7:30, take the free Alpine lot, and be boots-on and walking toward Center Village by 8:30. That gets you to American Flyer or American Eagle before the 9 a.m. start.
  • Check before you leave: Open PeakWait to see which Copper lifts are open and how fresh each wait report is. Early season, that tells you whether Super Bee is even an option.
  • Ride the chair with the shorter wait: Waits show as Moving, Short, Busy or Long on every lift. Take a look at the next chair, and report what you see when you ride, since the next skier is counting on it. PeakWait Plus ranks nearby resorts by snow and waits if Copper looks thin.

FAQ

What time does Copper Mountain open for skiing?

Copper's lift hours page lists base area lifts as open from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. That includes American Eagle, American Flyer, Super Bee, Woodward Express and Kokomo Express. Early in the season, only some lifts run, so check the resort's terrain status first.

Is parking free at Copper Mountain?

Yes, in two lots. The Alpine and Far East lots are free seven days a week during winter operating hours, according to Copper. The interior lots, including Ten Mile, Chapel, Union Creek, Beeler and Wheeler, are paid and first come, first served.

Which Copper village should I start in?

Center Village is the easiest default. It has American Eagle and American Flyer, food and a good meeting spot. Start in East Village if you want steeper terrain and Super Bee. Start in West Village if you are a beginner or have a lesson.

When do the upper lifts close at Copper?

Per Copper, Three Bears closes at 2:30 p.m. Storm King, Resolution, Blackjack and Mountain Chief close at 3 p.m. Timberline Express, Excelerator, Sierra, Lumberjack and Rendezvous close at 3:30 p.m. Do high terrain in the morning.
