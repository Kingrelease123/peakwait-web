Ski Singles Line Guide: How It Works and When It Saves You Time

The singles lane is the one lift shortcut that is not cutting. You fill the empty seats that groups leave behind, and on a busy day that can put you on a chair well ahead of the main maze. Here is how it works, where to look for one, how to behave in it, and a quick way to tell whether it will beat the main wait.

How a singles lane works

A chair or gondola cabin holds more people than most groups bring. A pair on a quad leaves two seats empty. A party of three leaves one. The singles lane exists to fill those holes.

Riders who are alone peel off into a narrow lane beside the main maze. When a group loads with room to spare, the attendant waves one or two singles forward to take the gaps. One ski blogger describes operators sending singles straight onto chairs while dozens of riders are still stuck in the main bottleneck. The chair leaves full, the lift moves more people per hour, and you skip the shuffle through the rope switchbacks.

Gondolas work the same way with bigger numbers. Vail's Gondola One carries ten people per cabin, so a group of four leaves six open seats for somebody.

Which resorts have singles lanes

The honest answer is that nobody keeps a master list, and I could not find a resort published one. Singles lanes are set lift by lift, not resort by resort, and resorts change them. PeakRankings notes that they tend to show up on popular, high capacity lifts that leave right from the base, because those lifts get the biggest rope mazes. Small resorts and lifts that rarely back up often have no singles lane at all. You walk up and the attendant pairs you with whoever is next.

Here is what I could actually back up:

  • Winter Park: a firsthand write up from several seasons ago described a singles lane on the base lifts it rode. It also named Gemini Express and Prospector Express as the lifts with the longest waits. Treat that as a hint, not a promise, and look for the sign.
  • Vail: I could not confirm singles lanes by lift. What guides do say is that the weekend waits that hurt are at the base gondolas, and that Riva Bahn Express out of Golden Peak and Born Free Express out of Lionshead are the usual ways around them.
  • Canada, 2020: ski hills across the country dropped their singles lines for the pandemic season. That is a good reminder that a resort can switch the lane off.

To spot one, look for a narrow lane next to the maze, a sign that says singles, or an attendant calling for singles. If you cannot tell, ask. Attendants would rather answer than watch you wander into the wrong lane.

How much time it actually saves

No one publishes a clean number, because it depends on who is standing ahead of you. Skiers who use the lane often wait far less than the main maze, according to PeakRankings. But a lane that is stacked with fifteen people can lose to a main maze of twenty.

A math writer named Daniel Carlander modeled it with leftover seats. The main maze gets the seats that groups fill. The singles lane gets the seats groups leave. Run his logic on a quad and you get a simple rule of thumb:

  • Parties of two: each chair leaves two seats for singles and uses two for the group. Pick the singles lane whenever fewer people are ahead of you there than in the main maze.
  • Parties of three: each chair leaves one seat for singles and uses three for the group. The singles lane needs to be about a third as long to win.
  • Parties of four: nothing is left over, so the singles lane tends to stall until a smaller group comes through.

On a gondola the same math favors singles even more, since ten seats rarely get filled exactly. So stand at the bottom, count heads in both lanes for ten seconds, and pick. Mornings with lots of families skew toward threes and fours. Midday with lots of pairs favors the singles lane.

Singles lane etiquette

The lane is for filling seats, not for gaming the system. A few rules keep you welcome.

  • Alone means alone: ride the singles lane when you are by yourself. Do not bring your group in as five singles and then expect to sit together.
  • Stay on your side: when a lift has two merging lanes, singles should join groups from their own side, unless nobody else is waiting there. Do not jump into the main maze as a single when a singles lane is open.
  • Invite people in: if your chair is not full and the singles lane is next to you, wave a single or two over. Empty seats are the whole problem.
  • Be ready: skis on, pass out, poles in hand before you reach the front. The lane only works if it keeps moving.
  • Talk on the chair: say hi, ask if the bar is okay to come down, and let the group choose where to sit.

When the singles lane does not help

Skip it when you are riding with a kid or a friend who needs you at the top. Skip it when both lanes are empty, because then you are just being pointlessly separate. Skip it when the singles lane is clearly longer than the main maze. And if the resort has paused it, as Canadian hills did in 2020, ask the attendant what the rule is today.

If you ski with a partner and do not mind sitting with strangers, splitting up in the singles lane can shorten your combined wait. Agree on a meeting spot at the top. Some attendants prefer pairs to stay together, so follow whatever they say.

FAQ

What is a singles line at a ski lift?

It is a separate lane for riders who are alone. The attendant sends singles forward to fill empty seats on chairs or gondola cabins, so the lift leaves full and you often board sooner than the main maze.

Does every ski resort have a singles lane?

No. Lanes are set lift by lift and resorts can pause or change them. They are most common on busy base lifts with big rope mazes. Look for a sign or ask the attendant.

Can I use the singles lane if I am skiing with friends?

Only if you are willing to ride separately. Splitting up can cut your combined wait, but you will sit with strangers, so pick a meeting spot at the top. Do not enter as a group and expect to sit together.

How many minutes does the singles lane save?

There is no reliable number, because it depends on who is ahead of you and how many seats groups leave empty. Count people in both lanes. With parties of two, the shorter lane wins. With parties of three, the singles lane needs to be about a third as long.

Is it rude to use the singles lane?

No, as long as you are actually alone and you wait your turn in it. It helps the lift run full. The rude move is joining the main maze as a single when a singles lane is open.
